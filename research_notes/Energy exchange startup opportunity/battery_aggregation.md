## Context: India storage pipeline, policy and cost baseline (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
India tendered a record ~130 GWh of storage in 2025 and awarded ~10.4 GW of standalone BESS, with discovered 2-hour tariffs collapsing to INR 1.48 lakh/MW/month and 4-hour to INR 2.85 lakh/MW/month; policy support is strong (INR 5,400 crore VGF for 30 GWh, ISTS waiver to June 2028, storage delicensed under Rule 18), but operational capacity is still small (5.3-8.5 GWh BESS) against a CEA need of 236 GWh BESS / 411 GWh total by FY32.

### Inferences
- At INR 1.1-1.4 crore/MWh landed, a 2-hour system costs ≈ INR 2.2-2.8 crore/MW and a 4-hour ≈ INR 4.4-5.6 crore/MW; the INR 18 lakh/MWh VGF covers only ~13-16% of 2-hour capex, so VGF alone does not rescue INR 1.48-1.9 lakh/MW/month bids.
- Tariff compression (INR 10.18/kWh → 2.1/kWh in three years) tracks the 45% y/y fall in Chinese stationary pack prices; India's bid stack is effectively a bet on continued import deflation, since domestic cell PLI has delivered 1.4 GWh.
- The gap between 185 GWh "under development" and 5.3-8.5 GWh operational means 2026-2028 will be the first real test of execution, financing and DISCOM offtake behaviour.

## Key Question 1: Realistic revenue per MW per year and payback in India under (a) tender tolling contracts and (b) merchant exchange arbitrage, vs UK/ERCOT benchmarks and their decline

### Takeaway
Indian tolling-style capacity contracts now pay INR 17.8-23 lakh/MW/yr for 2-hour and INR 34-52 lakh/MW/yr for 4-hour systems (12-16 year simple paybacks at the low end, which is why IEEFA flags 75% of 2-hour awards "at risk"); merchant arbitrage on IEX offers larger but volatile gross spreads (INR 5.5-7.5/kWh midday-to-evening in 2025, Ember 15-22% IRR for 1-2 hour merchant systems), while mature markets show the cautionary path: ERCOT fell from USD 193/kW-yr (2023) to ~USD 29/kW-yr (2025), CAISO from USD 80 to ~40/kW-yr, Australia FCAS from AUD 384k to 11k/MW-yr, and GB sits at £47-99k/MW/yr with ancillary saturation already priced in.

### Cited Findings

**(a) India contracted / tolling revenue**
- 2-hour standalone discovered tariffs 2025: INR 1.48 (APTRANSCO), 1.66 (MSEDCL), 1.85-1.89 (GUVNL) lakh/MW/month — see Context section sources ([IEEFA](https://ieefa.org/resources/viability-standalone-battery-energy-storage-tariffs-discovered-2025); [JMK Nov 2025](https://jmkresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/RE-Monthly-Update_November_2025-3.pdf))

### Inferences
- Annualised Indian contracted revenue (my arithmetic from the tariffs above): 2-hour INR 17.8 lakh/MW/yr at 1.48 lakh/month, INR 22.7 lakh at 1.89; 4-hour INR 34.2 lakh at 2.85, INR 37.7 lakh at 3.14, INR 52.1 lakh at 4.34. Against INR 2.2-2.8 crore/MW (2h) and 4.4-5.6 crore/MW (4h) capex, pre-O&M simple paybacks are ~12-16 years for the low bids and ~8.5-11 years for the NHPC Kerala tariff; VGF of INR 36 lakh/MW (2h) or 72 lakh/MW (4h) trims that by 1-2 years. These tariffs only clear with ~USD 70/kWh packs, 12-15+ year debt and ~1-cycle degradation assumptions.
- Merchant gross arbitrage (my arithmetic): a 2-hour, 1-cycle/day battery discharging ~660 MWh/MW/yr (330 days) at a INR 5.5/kWh gross spread ≈ INR 36 lakh/MW/yr; at INR 7.5/kWh ≈ INR 50 lakh/MW/yr; subtract ~15% round-trip losses on charging cost, exchange/transmission/DSM charges and degradation. That is roughly 2x the 2-hour contracted tariff, consistent with Ember's 15-22% IRR, but it rests on a 3-5 year window per IEX's own management and falling average DAM prices (−14% y/y in 9M FY26).
- Converted at indicative FX, mature-market merchant revenue in 2025 was: ERCOT ≈ INR 25 lakh/MW/yr, CAISO ≈ INR 34 lakh, GB ≈ INR 70-80 lakh, Australia ≈ INR 75 lakh (12-month avg), Germany ≈ INR 1.9-2.7 crore. India's merchant case (INR 36-50 lakh gross) sits between ERCOT/CAISO (post-saturation) and GB/AU; the ERCOT path (−85% in two years) is the realistic downside scenario once a few GW of merchant BESS target the same evening peak.
- The 8-hour PSP SECI price (INR 1.1 crore/MW/yr) versus 4-hour BESS at INR 0.34-0.52 crore/MW/yr implies storage buyers are paying roughly INR 1.1-1.4 lakh per MWh-of-duration per month regardless of technology, a useful benchmark for pricing storage-as-a-service.

## Key Question 2: Can an asset-light startup aggregate batteries it does not own in India? Contracts, metering, regulatory enablers

### Takeaway
The legal building blocks now exist (storage delicensed under Rule 18 of the Electricity Rules; ESS eligible for SRAS/TRAS under the 2022 Ancillary Services Regulations; stored energy tradeable on exchanges; GNA framework being amended for ESS), but there is no CERC-level aggregator licence or framework, ancillary participation by batteries is still at pilot scale, behind-the-meter sale-to-grid rules are state-level drafts, and most contracted BESS capacity is locked into 12-year DISCOM/SECI agreements with 95% availability and 2-cycle obligations, leaving only the ~merchant fleet, C&I behind-the-meter and EV-swap batteries as aggregatable pools.

- MoP amended Rule 18 of the Electricity Rules, 2005 (draft 11 Jun 2025; notified via G.S.R. 688(E), reported Sept 2025 / Jan 2026): ESS recognised as independent infrastructure asset; may be developed, owned, leased or operated by generators, transmission or distribution licensees, system operators, consumers, or any standalone developer, subject to technical standards, connectivity regulations and grid code; standalone ESS is a delicensed activity like generation; requires CEA registration — [Energetica](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/ministry-of-power-notifies-rules-to-expand-energy-storage-systems-ownership-and-usage); [K&S Partners](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/ministry-proposes-amendments-to-boost-ess/); [Power Peak Digest](https://powerpeakdigest.com/power-ministry-notifies-new-rules-for-energy-storage-systems/); [Neeti Niyaman](https://neetiniyaman.com/?p=1731)

- An asset-light aggregator in India today could legally (i) sign optimisation/tolling contracts with merchant standalone BESS owners (delicensed assets with GNA) and bid them into IEX DAM/RTM as the owner's trader or via a licensed trader, (ii) register as a demand-response/storage aggregator in states that have frameworks (Rajasthan 2026 first), and (iii) aggregate EV-swap or C&I behind-the-meter batteries for DISCOM peak-shaving à la Sheru/Tata Power. What it cannot yet do is sell aggregated sub-50 MW or behind-the-meter capacity into SRAS/TRAS (no aggregator eligibility; only a 150 MW pilot exists) or re-dispatch capacity that is under SECI/DISCOM BESPA contracts (2-cycle, 95% availability obligations).
- Metering/settlement for a multi-site virtual pool would require ABT-compliant SEMs at each site and a DSM-pool settlement entity; the only precedent is the Grid-India pilot procedure, so a startup would need a bespoke CERC/SERC order or a trader licence plus bilateral contracts.
- The "storage-as-a-service to generators" angle (absorbing curtailed RE) is constrained by Rajasthan's May 2026 draft that bars grid charging of co-located storage; the aggregator product would need to be structured as the generator's own storage (behind the pooling-station meter) or as a merchant buyer on exchange, not as third-party charging from the grid.

### Gaps
- No CERC regulation or draft specifically defining an "aggregator" licence for storage or DER at the inter-state level was found.
- Battery MW/MWh actually cleared in SRAS/TRAS were not found (Grid-India monthly reports not retrieved).
- Whether sub-50 MW standalone ESS can obtain GNA and exchange access on their own was not resolved beyond the draft IEGC text.
- Fee structures of UK optimisers (percentage of revenue, floors in £/MW/yr) were not found in public sources.

## Key Question 3: Size of the curtailed / excess renewable energy problem in India that storage could absorb

### Takeaway
Measured inter-state curtailment is still modest in energy terms (Ember: ~470 GWh in Q1 2026, ~300 GWh of it transmission-driven, with a 34 GWh single-day peak) but is concentrated in Rajasthan and Gujarat where midday curtailment of 50-100% is now routine for some plants and IEX midday prices hit INR 0-0.30/kWh; one estimate says ~10 GWh of batteries would eliminate current curtailment, i.e. the "excess RE" pool is a few hundred GWh/quarter today but growing fast with 43.7 GW of ISTS-connected solar.

### Cited Findings

- Ember: evening spreads INR 5.5/kWh (Jan 2025) and INR 7.5/kWh (Jun 2025) between midday and late evening — [Ember chapter 2](https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/the-age-of-storage-batteries-primed-for-indias-power-markets/chapter-2-understanding-pain-points)
- Policy response: CEA mandate proposal of 10% co-located storage (2h, 4h from Jul 2029) — [TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/storage/india-may-mandate-storage-solar-wind-projects-2027); Rajasthan draft bars grid charging of co-located storage (May 2026) — [JMK](https://jmkresearch.com/indias-emerging-merchant-bess-market-faces-its-first-regulatory-test/)

### Inferences
- 470 GWh/quarter ≈ 5 GWh/day average; a 2-hour fleet of ~2.5 GW (5 GWh) cycling once daily could in principle absorb the average, but the problem is peaky (34 GWh on the worst day) and location-specific (Rajasthan/Gujarat pooling stations), so storage sited at specific ISTS pooling stations matters more than aggregate GWh.
- At current scale the "curtailed energy" market is small in rupee terms: 470 GWh × INR 2.5/kWh (lost PPA revenue) ≈ INR 118 crore per quarter, consistent with the INR 250 crore industry-loss figure for Rajasthan over 2025. The larger prize is the price spread (INR 5.5-7.5/kWh) rather than the curtailed volume.
- Curtailment is rising with ISTS solar additions and transmission delays; the near-zero RTM episodes have moved from occasional (2025) to "multiple blocks in a month" (Apr 2026), which supports demand for storage but also signals that midday charging will be contested by many batteries once built.

### Gaps
- No Rajasthan- or Gujarat-specific GWh curtailment series from Grid-India/RLDCs was found; no official annual national curtailment figure for FY25 or FY26.
- No count of IEX time blocks with price ≤ INR 0.5/kWh per year.
- Intra-state (STU-level) curtailment not covered by Ember's inter-state estimate.

## Key Question 5: Main failure modes of storage businesses

### Takeaway
The documented failure modes are: ancillary-revenue cannibalisation as fleets saturate (ERCOT −85% in two years, NEM FCAS −97% since 2020, Germany FCR oversubscribed), spread compression from mild weather and fleet growth (CAISO record lows), aggressive/speculative Indian tender bids (75% of 2-hour awards "at risk"), offtaker delays and cancellations (CERC cancelled SECI's first BESS tender after a 245-day PPA lag), DISCOM payment arrears (INR 581 billion total dues June 2025), regulatory reversals (grid-charging bans), performance penalties (2x capacity charge LDs, degradation formulas) and catastrophic fire (Moss Landing destroyed ~80% of a 300 MW/1,200 MWh phase).
