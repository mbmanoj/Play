# India: Power Market Structure and Regulatory Framework for an Energy-Exchange / Flexibility Startup (as of 14 Nov 2026)

Research-method note for the report writer: all findings below come from web-search result summaries and snippets gathered on 14 Nov 2026. Primary government domains (cercind.gov.in, powermin.gov.in, cea.nic.in, grid-india.in) and most Indian trade-press domains (powerline.net.in, solarquarter.com, mercomindia.com, business-standard.com, indianinfrastructure.com, legitquest.com, jmkresearch.com) could not be fetched directly from this session's network, so regulation text was not read in full; where a figure rests on a secondary summary or a snippet of a CERC order this is stated. ## Key Question 1: What is the lowest-friction legal route today for a startup to (a) trade on IEX on behalf of generators, (b) aggregate consumers or batteries, (c) sell flexibility services to DISCOMs or Grid-India? Which licences or memberships are needed and what do they cost?

### Takeaway
Route (a) is the most mature: trading on an exchange "on behalf of" others requires an inter-State trading licence from CERC under the 2020 Trading Licence Regulations (category-based net worth from Rs 2 crore for Category V up to Rs 100 crore for Category I) plus membership of a power exchange under the CERC Power Market Regulations 2021; a generator can alternatively trade directly as a client of an existing trader-member. Route (b) and (c) have a legal opening at the inter-State level only via the CERC Ancillary Services Regulations 2022 (demand response and storage, standalone or aggregated, minimum 1 MW, with SCADA telemetry), while retail-level aggregation is governed by a patchwork of new state regulations (Maharashtra 2024, Assam draft 2024, Karnataka draft 2025, Rajasthan 2025/2026) in which the aggregator is an entity registered with or appointed by the DISCOM, not an independently licensed market participant; no national "aggregator licence" exists.

### Cited Findings

#### Structure of the exchanges (IEX, PXIL, HPX)
- Three exchanges traded a combined 168.55 BU in FY2025-26; IEX accounted for 84% (141.14 BU), PXIL 18.35 BU (11%) and HPX 9.06 BU (5%), per the CEA Annual Market Monitoring Report as summarised by Indian Infrastructure (July 2026) — [Indian Infrastructure, "Trading Trends: Shift to market-based solutions", 3 Jul 2026](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2026/07/03/trading-trends-shift-to-market-based-solutions/); underlying CEA report — [CEA dashboard report PDF](https://cea.nic.in/api/dashboardReports/view-pdf/en/40c5fda7-9e60-4d10-bb0e-8b529b5a629a)
- IEX's own management guidance put its market share at 83-85% in Q3 FY25 and 83% in Q2 FY25 — [Quartr, IEX Q3 FY25 earnings summary](https://quartr.com/events/indian-energy-exchange-limited-iex-q3-24-25_FkrfEE5J); [Quartr, IEX Q2 FY25](https://quartr.com/events/indian-energy-exchange-limited-iex-q4-24-25_FkrfEFAo)
- An older (undated, likely FY24-era) secondary source put IEX at ~90% and PXIL ~8% — [Sharescart](https://www.sharescart.com/unlisted-shares/articles/india-s-power-market-shake-up-can-hpx-challenge-iex-s-monopoly/); the 84% CEA-based figure reconciles with the volume totals and is the more reliable for FY26.
- HPX crossed a cumulative 100 MU of trading in or around November 2025 — [Mojo4Industry, 5 Nov 2025](https://www.mojo4industry.com/hindustan-power-exchange-crosses-100-mus-of-power-trading/) (note: this is a company-milestone figure and appears inconsistent in scale with the CEA FY26 HPX total of 9.06 BU; treat the CEA figure as authoritative and verify the Mojo4Industry unit).
- PTC India is a co-promoter of HPX; PTC's own trading volumes rose 12.15% / exchange volumes rose 21.90% in FY26 (company-level, not HPX volume) — [Multibagg, PTC FY26](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/ptc-india-fy26-trading-volumes-cmvoys15i000832pqmh86hexp)
- IEX segments operating: DAM (incl. HP-DAM), RTM, TAM, G-DAM, G-TAM, REC, ESCerts; FY26 DAM incl. HP-DAM volume 62.78 BU; RTM is the fastest-growing segment (RTM hit record highs in April 2026 as IEX monthly volume reached 12,341 MU, +16.6% YoY) — [Energetica India, IEX FY26](https://energetica-india.net/news/iex-reports-141-bu-fy26-volume-rec-trade-surges-119-9-percent-yoy-in-march); [Sahi, IEX April 2026](https://www.sahi.com/news/iex-april-2026-power-volume-jumps-16-6-to-12-341-mu-as-rtm-hits-record-highs-4616-PE1_COR)
- Currently permitted sellers in the High Price DAM (HP-DAM) segment are gas stations on imported RLNG/naphtha, imported-coal stations and battery storage; CERC has proposed adding pumped storage plants (suo motu) and Greenko petitioned for PSP inclusion — [Power Peak Digest, "CERC proposes allowing pumped storage plants in HP-DAM"](https://powerpeakdigest.com/cerc-pumped-storage-plants-hp-dam/)
- IEX petitioned CERC (August 2025) for "Peak DAM" and "Peak RTM" segments intended for flexible resources such as BESS; CER-IITK's comments said limiting participation to storage is "an artificial barrier to entry" and suggested widening eligibility — [CER IIT Kanpur comments on IEX Peak DAM/RTM petition](https://cer.iitk.ac.in/odf_assets/upload_files/blog_CER-IITK_Comments_on_IEX_Petition_for_Peak_DAM_and_Peak_RTM1.pdf)
- HPX petitioned (December 2025) to introduce Green RTM and HP-RTM; it recommended technology-agnostic "energy storage system" eligibility rather than battery-only — [CER IIT Kanpur blog on HPX Green RTM / HP-RTM](https://cer.iitk.ac.in/odf_assets/upload_files/blog_HPX_Introduction_of_Green_RTM_and_HP_RTM.pdf)
- In 2026 CERC hearings, counsel argued the existing DAM price cap "is rendering participation by energy storage systems in the DAM commercially unviable" — [CERC Record of Proceedings 194/MP/2026, 23 Jun 2026](https://cercind.gov.in/2026/ROP/194-MP-2026_230626.pdf); related ROP — [60/MP/2026, 13 May 2026](https://cercind.gov.in/2026/ROP/60-MP-2026_130526.pdf)

#### CERC Power Market Regulations 2021 (PMR 2021)
- CERC notified the Power Market Regulations, 2021 in February 2021, replacing the 2010 regulations; they came into force on 15 Aug 2021 — [Power Line, "Policy Revamp: CERC notifies Power Market Regulations, 2021", 7 Mar 2021](https://powerline.net.in/2021/03/07/policy-revamp/); [Indian Infrastructure, 13 May 2021](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2021/05/13/cerc-issues-power-market-regulations-2021/); regulation text — [LegitQuest copy of PMR 2021](https://www.legitquest.com/act/central-electricity-regulatory-commission-power-market-regulations-2021/CBB3)
- A "client" is defined as a person who has executed an agreement with a member of a Power Exchange for dealing with or clearing through such member — [LegitQuest copy of PMR 2021](https://www.legitquest.com/act/central-electricity-regulatory-commission-power-market-regulations-2021/CBB3)
- Secondary summary of the 2020 draft: exchange membership categorised into trader member, proprietary member and facilitator member — [Mondaq, "Highlights of the proposed Power Market Regulations 2020"](https://www.mondaq.com/india/contracts-and-commercial-law/976634/highlights-of-the-proposed-power-market-regulations-2020) (draft-stage summary; verify against notified text)
- Exchange transactions are tied to the CERC Open Access Regulations 2008 categories (advance scheduling, FCFS, day-ahead bilateral, contingency) — [Mondaq summary](https://www.mondaq.com/india/contracts-and-commercial-law/976634/highlights-of-the-proposed-power-market-regulations-2020)
- Minimum volume quotation in exchange segments is 1 MW with 1 MWh lot size (IEX petition); CERC in a G-TAM order said the 1 MW minimum was set "keeping in view the large size of hydro generating stations" — [IEX petition, 14 Aug 2023](https://www.iexindia.com/pdf/IEX%20Petition_14082023.pdf); [CERC order 169/MP/2021](https://cercind.gov.in/2022/orders/169-MP-2021.pdf)

#### Trading licence (CERC Procedure, Terms and Conditions for grant of Trading Licence Regulations, 2020)
- Category I: CERC orders describe a Rs 100 crore net worth requirement (proviso to Regulation 9(2)); one scanned text gives Rs 75 crore for applicants proposing >10,000 MU — [CERC order 15/SM/2020](https://cercind.gov.in/2020/orders/15-SM-2020.pdf); [Scribd copy of regulation](https://www.scribd.com/document/509795817/154) (figures conflict: Rs 75 crore vs Rs 100 crore; verify against the 31 Jan 2020 notification)
- Category III: minimum net worth increased to Rs 20 crore under the 2020 regulations — [CERC order 42/TD/2021](https://cercind.gov.in/2021/orders/42-TD-2021.pdf)
- Category IV: Rs 10 crore net worth plus minimum current-ratio requirement — [CERC order 152/TD/2021](https://cercind.gov.in/2021/orders/152-TD-2021.pdf)
- Category V: Rs 2 crore net worth plus minimum current ratio — [CERC order 557/TD/2020](https://cercind.gov.in/2020/orders/557-TD-2020.pdf)
- Where current ratio falls below 1:1, licensee must maintain additional net worth of 100% of the stipulated amount; downgrading requires an audited special balance sheet; CERC gave existing licensees until 31 Oct 2020 to comply, and has rejected downgrade applications lacking the balance sheet — [CERC order 8/SM/2021](https://cercind.gov.in/2021/orders/8-SM-2021.pdf); [CERC order 14/TD/2026](https://cercind.gov.in/2026/orders/14-TD-2026.pdf); [CERC order 294/TD/2026](https://cercind.gov.in/2026/Orders/294-TD-2026-F.pdf)
- CERC continues to grant and process trading licences in 2025-26 (e.g., petitions 849/TD/2025, 313/TD/2025, 14/TD/2026, 294/TD/2026) — [CERC 849/TD/2025](https://cercind.gov.in/2025/orders/849-TD-2025.pdf); [CERC 313/TD/2025](https://cercind.gov.in/2025/orders/313-TD-2025-I.pdf)

#### Who can be a client / open access thresholds
- The Green Energy Open Access Rules (June 2022) made consumers with 100 kW load eligible for green open access (down from the 1 MW general open-access threshold), but did not clarify single vs aggregated connections — [Mongabay India, Jun 2023](https://india.mongabay.com/2023/06/power-ministry-drives-clean-energy-open-access-empowering-small-consumers/)
- The Second Amendment Rules (May 2023) clarified eligibility as contracted demand/sanctioned load of 100 kW or more, through a single connection or multiple connections aggregating 100 kW in the same electricity division of a DISCOM; standby charge threshold raised from 10% to 25% of energy charges — [India Environment Portal, GEOA Second Amendment Rules 2023](https://www.indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/reports-and-documents/electricity-promoting-renewable-energy-through-green-energy-open-access-second-amendment-rules-2023); [Indian Infrastructure, 23 May 2023](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2023/05/23/mop-notifies-electricity-promoting-renewable-energy-through-green-energy-open-access-second-amendment-rules-2023/)
- State adoption is uneven: Haryana HERC amended its 2023 GEOA regulations (approved 25 Feb 2025) to allow aggregation of connections to reach 100 kW — [Renewable Watch, 4 Mar 2025](https://renewablewatch.in/2025/03/04/haryana-amends-green-energy-open-access-regulations/); Assam amended its 2024 rules (Sept 2025) — [SolarQuarter, 25 Sep 2025](https://solarquarter.com/2025/09/25/assam-electricity-commission-amends-2024-open-access-rules-to-promote-renewable-energy/); Delhi DERC issued a draft (comments due 5 May 2026) to drop the 11 kV connection-voltage requirement and apply the 100 kW threshold — [Mercom, "Delhi proposes expanding green energy open access eligibility"](https://mercomindia.com/delhi-proposes-expanding-green-energy-open-access-eligibility); [Energetica India](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/derc-proposes-removing-voltage-level-restriction-for-green-energy-open-access-projects)
- Consumers below 100 kW have no open-access route; the search found no regulation allowing sub-100 kW consumers to be exchange clients (see Gaps).

#### Ancillary services (CERC Ancillary Services Regulations 2022)
- Regulations cover PRAS, SRAS, TRAS plus voltage control and black start; they apply to regional entities including "entities having energy storage resources and entities capable of providing demand response" — [Power Line, 9 Feb 2022](https://powerline.net.in/2022/02/09/cerc-notifiesancillary-services-regulations-2022/); [Grid-India copy of regulations](https://grid-india.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Ancillary-Service-Regulations-2022.pdf)
- An SRAS provider may be a generating station, an entity with energy storage, or an entity capable of demand response, "on standalone or aggregated basis", connected to ISTS or intra-State; minimum response 1 MW; metering and SCADA telemetry required — [TeamLease RegTech summary](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/16242/cerc-ancillary-services-regulations-2022); [IEX "Innovation" Mar 2022](https://www.iexindia.com/pdf/Innovation-March-2022.pdf)
- SRAS is performance-based (despatch and compensation based on ramp); TRAS is bid-based with energy-up/energy-down bids in INR/MWh for an offered MW volume — [IEX Innovation Mar 2022](https://www.iexindia.com/pdf/Innovation-March-2022.pdf); [CER-IITK blog on draft AS regulations](https://cer.iitk.ac.in/odf_assets/upload_files/CER-IITK-Draft_Ancillary_Services__Blog_v3.02.pdf)
- Effective dates conflict across sources: regulations in force 31 Jan 2022; TRAS provisions notified to commence 1 Apr 2023 (CERC notification) / 1 May 2023 (Indian Infrastructure) / 1 Jun 2023 (later CERC order) — [CERC TRAS notification](https://www.cercind.gov.in/Regulations/Ancillary-Services-TRAS-Notification.pdf); [Indian Infrastructure, "CERC notifies applicability of TRAS from May 1, 2023"](https://indianinfrastructure.com/?p=119724); [CERC order 07/SM/2023](https://cercind.gov.in/2023/orders/07-SM-2023.pdf)
- Stakeholder comments on the 2021 draft (CESC, AEEE, IESA) asked for a formal "demand response aggregator" definition and for DER aggregation (distributed storage, EVs, DR) to meet the 1 MW minimum; one proposed the aggregator be scheduled by the SLDC as a virtual load/generator; whether the final text adopted these was not confirmed — [CESC comments](https://cercind.gov.in/2021/draft_reg/comment_ASR/27.%20CESC's%20submission%20on%20draft%20Ancillary%20Services%20Regulation-30-06-21.pdf); [AEEE comments](https://cercind.gov.in/2021/draft_reg/comment_ASR/28.%20AEEE%20comments%20on%20Draft%20Ancillary%20Services%20Regulation%202021.pdf); [IESA comments on draft IEGC 2022](https://cercind.gov.in/2022/draft_reg/IEGC-PPT-2022/IESA%20Comments%20on%20Draft%20IEGC%202022.pdf)
- Grid-India/POSOCO has an approved procedure for scheduling, metering, accounting and settlement of the ancillary portion of a 150 MW/300 MWh BESS pilot project — [POSOCO procedure](https://posoco.in/english-approved-procedure-for-scheduling-metering-accounting-and-settlement-for-ancillary-portion-150mw-300mwh-of-bess-pilot-project)
- A 2025 CER-IITK presentation discusses "DSO evolution" and the emerging landscape for ancillary services at distribution level — [CER-IITK, DSO Evolution 2025](https://cer.iitk.ac.in/assets/downloads/CBP/presentation/Day2/DSO_Evolution_and_Emerging_Landscape_for_Ancillary_Services2025.pdf)
- Madhya Pradesh MPERC floated draft state-level Ancillary Services regulations in 2024 — [CER-IITK blog on draft MPERC AS regulations 2024](https://cer.iitk.ac.in/odf_assets/upload_files/blog/Draft_MPERC_AS_regulations_2024.pdf)

#### State demand-response / aggregator frameworks (route for selling flexibility to DISCOMs)
- Maharashtra: MERC (Demand Flexibility and Demand Side Management – Implementation Framework, Cost-Effectiveness Assessment, and EM&V) Regulations 2024 are among the first demand flexibility regulations in India; they create a Demand Flexibility Portfolio Obligation (DFPO) requiring DISCOMs to shift a specified share of peak demand; a Bhandup pilot (Feb 2024) shifted 345 kW daily over three hours — [Down To Earth, "Demand flexibility is key..."](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/demand-flexibility-is-key-to-accelerating-renewable-integration-in-india)
- Assam AERC released draft Demand Response Regulations 2024, defining an aggregator as "an entity registered with the Distribution Licensee to provide aggregation of ... demand response services" — [CER-IITK comments on AERC draft DR Regulation 2024](https://cer.iitk.ac.in/odf_assets/upload_files/blog_CER_IITK_Comments_on_AERC_Draft_Demand_Response_Regulation_2024.pdf); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/demand-flexibility-is-key-to-accelerating-renewable-integration-in-india)
- Karnataka KERC released a draft Framework for Demand Flexibility/DSM Regulations 2025 — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/demand-flexibility-is-key-to-accelerating-renewable-integration-in-india)
- Rajasthan RERC's 2025 draft BESS regulations define a "Distributed Energy Resources Aggregator (DERA)" as an entity registered/appointed with/by the distribution licensee — [RERC BESS draft (JMK Research copy)](https://jmkresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/RERC_BESSdraft.pdf); [CER-IITK blog, RERC BESS 2025](https://cer.iitk.ac.in/odf_assets/upload_files/blog_RERC_BESS_2025.pdf); final regulations — [RERC BESS Regulations 2025](https://jmkresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/BESS-Regulations-2025_rajasthan.pdf)
- Rajasthan notified a demand-flexibility obligation in 2026 (per secondary source); its statement of reasons shows stakeholders debated whether grid-connected distributed solar can count in an aggregator portfolio and whether DG sets should be excluded — [RERC order (JMK copy), Apr 2026](https://jmkresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/rerc_Order-2.pdf); [Energy Decoded, "The Business of Flexibility"](https://energyleaks.substack.com/p/the-business-of-flexibility)
- Delhi: Tata Power-DDL ran a behavioural demand response programme with AutoGrid, with results to be shared with DERC to design incentives; a vendor case study claims growth from 2,000 to 100,000 customers in three years from ~1 MW of initial load shed — [ETN News](https://etn.news/buzz/tata-power-ddl-us-based-autogrid-partners-to-install-ai-based-ems-in-delhi?format=amp); [Uplight blog](https://uplight.com/blog/empowering-india-energy-revolution-ai-prosumers/) (vendor-sourced; treat with caution)
- Mumbai: Tata Power's AutoGrid-based programme targeted 55,000 residential and 6,000 C&I consumers for 75 MW peak reduction in six months — [Uplight blog](https://uplight.com/blog/empowering-india-energy-revolution-ai-prosumers/) (vendor-sourced)
- A secondary analysis states India has no commercial virtual power plant, no Indian statute or notified regulation uses the term "VPP", and every documented programme is described by its operator as demand response — [Uplift Growth, "Virtual Power Plants"](https://upliftgrowth.in/systems-analysis/virtual-power-plants/) (secondary; unverified)

#### Peer-to-peer trading pilots

### Inferences
- For route (a), a startup that lacks Rs 2 crore+ net worth and licence lead-time can start as a "client" of an existing trader-member (PTC, Manikaran, etc.) and move to its own Category V/IV licence later; the licence process is active (multiple 2025-26 CERC TD petitions) but enforcement of net-worth and current-ratio tests is strict.
- For route (b)/(c), the only inter-State door is the SRAS/TRAS framework (1 MW aggregated, SCADA telemetry, regional-entity status). Practically, a behind-the-meter aggregator today must contract with a DISCOM under a state DF/DSM regulation (Maharashtra is furthest along), because the state drafts define the aggregator as a DISCOM-registered entity rather than a market participant.
- The 100 kW GEOA threshold means an aggregator of C&I rooftops/loads could reach exchange markets via open access only when customers (or aggregated connections in one division) are 100 kW+, and only where the SERC has adopted the rule; residential participation remains gated by smart meters and DISCOM programmes.

## Key Question 2: What specific regulatory changes between 2024 and 2026 open or close doors for a flexibility/VPP/storage aggregator in India?

### Takeaway
2024-26 brought a cluster of enabling but mostly incomplete changes: the DSM Regulations 2024 (tighter deviation bands, 15-minute settlement framework), market coupling (ordered July 2025, draft regulations April 2026 making Grid-India sole MCO, final regulations still pending as of IEX's 30 Oct 2026 call), SEBI-regulated electricity futures (July 2025), the draft Electricity (Amendment) Bill 2025 (Oct 2025, not yet enacted), the draft National Electricity Policy 2026 (Jan 2026) explicitly calling for aggregators, the draft Rights of Consumers Amendment Rules 2026 (Mar 2026) creating a demand-response framework at SERC level, and a CERC discussion paper (May 2026) on 5-minute RTM scheduling. Doors that remain closed: no aggregator licence, no notified VPP framework, DAM price caps that exchanges' own petitions say make BESS uneconomic, and smart-meter rollout at ~29% of sanctioned numbers.

### Cited Findings

#### Deviation Settlement Mechanism 2024 and settlement granularity
- CERC issued the draft DSM Regulations on 30 Apr 2024; the final regulations were published in the Gazette on 21 Aug 2024 (some commentary dates issue to 5 Aug 2024) and replaced the DSM Regulations 2022 — [Prayas comments on draft DSM 2024](https://cercind.gov.in/2024/draft_reg/comments-DSM%20Reg2024/Comments/78%20Prayas.pdf); [CERC Gazette G-766, 15 Sep 2024](https://cercind.gov.in/Regulations/G-766-15Sep2024.pdf); [Energetica India](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/cerc-introduces-new-deviation-settlement-mechanism-regulations)
- Main commencement 16 Sep 2024; Regulation 8(8) (charge for deviation from injection of infirm power) effective 1 Dec 2024; First Amendment in force 23 Dec 2024 — [Legality Simplified, timelines](https://legalitysimplified.com/?p=56915); [Legality Simplified, Clause 8 commencement](https://www.legalitysimplified.com/?p=59449); [CERC draft First Amendment](https://cercind.gov.in/2024/draft_reg/DR-DSM_First%20Amendment.pdf)
- Tolerance bands cut from 10% to 5% for solar/hybrid and 15% to 10% for wind; Prayas estimated the share of time blocks within limits could fall 20-30% without pooling — [Prayas comments (78)](https://cercind.gov.in/2024/draft_reg/comments-DSM%20Reg2024/Comments/78%20Prayas.pdf); [Prayas comments (44), 2025](https://cercind.gov.in/2025/draft_reg/comments-DSM24/44.%20Prayas.pdf)
- Charges are differentiated by seller type; provisions cover infirm power, start-up power, cross-border, forced outages, payment timelines, LPS and LCs — [K&S Partners newsletter](https://ksandk.com/md/newsletter/cerc-deviation-settlement-mechanism-2024/)
- A CERC order of 10 Sep 2025 proposed how to set the normal-rate parameter "X" for wind and solar sellers from 1 Apr 2026 under Regulation 6(2)(b) — [Comment by Shanti Prasad on CERC draft, 2025](https://cercind.gov.in/2025/draft_reg/comments-DSM24/24.%20Mr.%20Shanti%20Prasad.pdf)
- Delhi DERC aligned intra-State DSM with CERC rates effective 16 Sep 2024 — [SolarQuarter, 16 Sep 2024](https://solarquarter.com/2024/09/16/implementation-of-intra-state-deviation-settlement-mechanism-in-delhi-as-per-cerc-regulations-2024/)
- 5-minute settlement: the Forum of Regulators' Feb 2018 sub-group report covered 5-minute scheduling, metering, accounting and settlement; CERC's 2018 suo motu order (07/SM/2018) said fast-response technologies like battery storage and demand response would need shorter despatch and settlement periods — [FoR Sub-Group Report](https://forumofregulators.gov.in/Data/Reports/5.pdf); [CERC 2018 order](https://cercind.gov.in/2018/orders/08.pdf)

## Key Question 3: Where are the clearest regulatory gaps (no settlement for behind-the-meter flexibility, no aggregator licence, DISCOM opposition to open access via surcharges)?

### Takeaway
The clearest gaps are: (1) no national aggregator/VPP licence or registration category—state drafts make the aggregator a DISCOM-registered entity, so market access depends on DISCOM consent; (2) the 1 MW minimum and SCADA requirement in CERC ancillary services keep small DERs out unless aggregated and telemetered; (3) exchange price caps and segment eligibility rules (HP-DAM/Peak DAM restricted to listed seller types) that exchanges and commentators say make storage participation uneconomic; (4) open access below 100 kW is unavailable and state adoption of the 100 kW rule is uneven; (5) smart-meter coverage (~29% of sanctioned) and incomplete ToD roll-out limit residential settlement; (6) no public real-time data API from Grid-India/SLDCs.

### Inferences
- A startup cannot today be paid directly by Grid-India for sub-1 MW behind-the-meter flexibility; the only counterparties are DISCOMs (under state DF/DSM regulations) or, at 1 MW+ with telemetry, the SRAS/TRAS mechanism. - Data access is report-based (PDF/CSV), so a startup will need to scrape Grid-India/IEX/SLDC pages or buy data; the 2026 draft data-sharing framework is the signal to watch.

## Key Question 5: What are the near-term policy signals (MoP roadmap, National Electricity Plan, CERC staff papers) about demand-side flexibility and distributed resources?

### Takeaway
The strongest signals are the draft National Electricity Policy 2026 (released 21 Jan 2026) which directs Central and State Commissions to "introduce aggregators" for prosumers, storage and EVs with no charges beyond network costs and to procure ancillary services market-wide with consumer participation via aggregators; the draft Rights of Consumers Amendment Rules 2026 (demand-response framework at SERC level); CERC's April 2026 capacity-market staff paper and May 2026 5-minute RTM paper; and the June 2026 draft National Electricity Data Sharing Framework. Civil-society proposals (RMI: 1 GW of third-party aggregated demand flexibility by 2027) and state DF obligations (Maharashtra 2024, Rajasthan 2026) indicate direction, but all central instruments remain drafts as of Nov 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Draft National Electricity Policy 2026, released for consultation 21 Jan 2026 (MoP PDF carries a June 2026 upload path, so a revised version may exist): "Central and State Commissions must introduce aggregators to pool demand and supply, improve market access for prosumers, storage, and EVs, and optimize distributed energy"; aggregator participation to carry no additional charges beyond network costs; market-based procurement of ancillary services with consumer participation via demand response individually or through aggregators — [MoP draft NEP 2026 PDF](https://www.powermin.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/06/0593dd0f9b721abdb5227d7ddf5ca439.pdf); [RAP comments on draft NEP 2026](https://www.raponline.org/knowledge-center/comments-to-indias-ministry-of-power-on-the-draft-national-electricity-policy-2026/)
- Draft Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Amendment Rules 2026 (Mar 2026): SERCs to specify DR provider eligibility, incentives, communication protocols and procedures — [Power Line, 17 Mar 2026](https://powerline.net.in/2026/03/17/ministry-of-power-issues-draft-electricity-rights-of-consumers-amendment-rules-2026/)
- CERC staff paper on capacity market (Apr 2026, comments to 27 May 2026) — [Power Line](https://powerline.net.in/2026/05/06/cerc-issues-staff-paper-on-capacity-market-for-electricity-in-india/); [Power Peak Digest](https://powerpeakdigest.com/cerc-capacity-market-framework-proposal-2026/)

### Inferences
- The policy direction is unambiguous (aggregators, DR, storage in markets, faster settlement), but every national instrument is at draft/staff-paper stage; a founder should expect 12-24 months before any of them is binding, and should position in states with notified DF regulations (Maharashtra, Rajasthan) and in the 1 MW+ SRAS/TRAS niche meanwhile.
- Market coupling plus a single-MCO Grid-India and a national data portal would, if implemented, make Grid-India (not IEX) the key counterparty for data and clearing—worth designing integrations to be exchange-agnostic.
