## Key Question 1: How much does a 100 MW solar or wind plant in India lose per year to DSM penalties, curtailment and poor exchange bidding, and how much can software recover?

### Takeaway
Under the pre-April-2026 regime, DSM charges cost a well-run Indian RE plant roughly 1-3% of revenue (about INR 0.04-0.05/kWh solar, INR 0.07-0.08/kWh wind, i.e. ~INR 0.8-1 crore/yr for 100 MW solar and ~INR 2 crore/yr for 100 MW wind); the April 2026 band tightening is estimated by the wind industry to raise DSM charges 35-40%, and the X-factor glide path to 0% by April 2031 will raise them further. Curtailment losses are lumpy and location-specific (Rajasthan developers reported up to INR 250 crore of losses across ~4 GW over Mar-Aug 2025, i.e. ~INR 6 crore per 100 MW in six months), while merchant solar's biggest "loss" is structural price capture (IEX solar capture rate fell from ~INR 5/kWh in FY23 to INR 2.23/kWh in FY26) which software alone cannot fix without storage or shape management.

### Cited Findings

**DSM regulatory baseline (what drives the penalty)**
- CERC DSM Regulations 2024 were notified 5 Aug 2024 and became effective 16 Sep 2024; for wind/solar/hybrid sellers deviation charges are delinked from grid frequency and symmetrical within the volume limits — [Energetica India](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/cerc-notifies-phased-x-factor-reduction-for-wind-and-solar-tightens-deviation-bands-from-april-2026); [TTA client alert Aug 2024](https://tta.in/?p=11916); [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/?p=12678)
- From 1 Apr 2026 the revenue-neutral (penalty-free) band narrows from ±10% to ±5% for solar/hybrid and from ±15% to ±10% for wind (Regulation 8(4) of DSM Regulations 2024) — [Prayas comment to CERC](https://cercind.gov.in/2025/draft_reg/comments-DSM24/44.%20Prayas.pdf); [PV Magazine India, 2 Jun 2026](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2026/06/02/scheduling-discipline-at-a-judicial-crossroads-indias-evolving-deviation-settlement-mechanism/); [Mercom](https://www.mercomindia.com/cerc-notifies-phased-move-to-schedule-based-deviation-for-wind-solar-under-dsm)
- CERC order in Petition 9/SM/2025 dated 31 Mar 2026 sets the "X" factor (share of Available Capacity vs Scheduled Generation in the deviation denominator). As X falls, the same MW deviation attracts a larger charge. Glide path per JSA: solar/hybrid X = 100% (FY27), 90% (FY28), 75% (FY29), 55% (FY30); wind X = 100%, 95%, 85%, 65% for the same years; X reaches 0% (pure schedule-based) by April 2031 — [JSA Prism Apr 2026](https://www.jsalaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/JSA-Prism-ElectricityPower-April-2026-CERC-Order.Final_.pdf); [SolarQuarter 1 Apr 2026](https://solarquarter.com/2026/04/01/cerc-announces-phased-dsm-reform-for-wind-and-solar-tightens-deviation-norms-by-2031/); [Powerline May 2026](https://powerline.net.in/2026/05/05/stricter-norms-moving-towards-a-schedule-based-deviation-regime/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/renewables/1779874/central-electricity-regulatory-commission-sets-trajectory-for-deviation-computation-in-renewable-energy-sector)
- Prayas proposed a faster path (X = 40-50% solar, 60-70% wind for FY2026-28) based on 15-minute DSM data for FY24 and FY25 covering >70% of ISTS-connected wind/solar in Western and Southern regions — [Prayas](https://cercind.gov.in/2025/draft_reg/comments-DSM24/44.%20Prayas.pdf)
- State-level F&S regulations (intra-state plants): most states allow ±15% error without penalty, some ±10% (CEEW, 2020) — [CEEW](https://www.ceew.in/gfc/quick-reads/analysis/streamlining-forecasting-scheduling-dsm-regulations-for-wind-in-india)
- Rajasthan High Court upheld the state's forecasting & scheduling regulations against an Indian Wind Power Association challenge over high penalties and QCA forecasting ability — [Bridge to India](https://bridgetoindia.com/forecasting-and-scheduling-regulations-take-hold-in-india/)

**Magnitude of DSM charges**
- Industry estimate (2019-20): average DSM penalty of INR 0.04-0.05/kWh for solar and INR 0.07-0.08/kWh for wind at pooling-station/plant level, plus QCA fees and infrastructure; deviations above 15% can cost INR 0.50-1.50/kWh — [Bridge to India](https://bridgetoindia.com/forecasting-regulations-a-tough-proposition-for-developers/)
- Worked example in 2026 commentary: a wind project with INR 2.80/kWh tariff currently loses ~2.8% to deviation charges, i.e. an effective tariff of INR 2.72/kWh (INR 0.08/kWh) — [Energetica India](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/cerc-notifies-phased-x-factor-reduction-for-wind-and-solar-tightens-deviation-bands-from-april-2026)
- IWPA calculated that tightening the wind band from ±15% to ±10% raises DSM charges by 35-40% — [IWPA comment to CERC](https://cercind.gov.in/2025/draft_reg/comments-DSM24/15.%20IWPA.pdf)
- NGEL data: at its Fatehgarh wind project 39.7% of 15-minute blocks deviate beyond ±5%, 19.6% beyond ±10%, 10.5% beyond ±15% — [NGEL comment to CERC](https://cercind.gov.in/2025/draft_reg/comments-DSM24/25.%20NGEL.pdf)
- Tata Power notes DSM impact "reduces significantly" when generators aggregate at a pooling station — [Tata Power comment](https://cercind.gov.in/2025/draft_reg/comments-DSM24/35.%20Tata%20Power.pdf)
- Grid-India/RLDC weekly DSM accounts list payable/receivable per entity including RE generators (e.g. ERLDC accounts) but no annual RE-only total is published — [ERPC DSM accounts Jul 2025](https://erpc.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/DSM-SRAS-TRAS-and-SCUC-Accounts-for-the-period-from-07.07.2025-to-13.07.2025..pdf)

**Curtailment**
- Rajasthan: ~3-4 GW of solar curtailed since March 2025, industry losses of up to INR 250 crore by Aug 2025; curtailment share rose from 8.5% (March) to 51.5% (August) — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/rajasthan-curtails-4-gw-of-solar-capacity-since-march-industry-incurs-losses-of-up-to-rs-250-crore); [Mercom](https://mercomindia.com/rajasthan-curtails-4-gw-of-renewables-as-transmission-delays-mount)
- NSEFI clarified an earlier "1,408 GW cumulative curtailment" report was inaccurate — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/transmission-trap)
- Ember: India curtailed ~2.3 TWh of solar between May and Dec 2025 via emergency TRAS instructions; ~40% in October alone (~0.9 TWh); on some December 2025 days ~4 GW of solar was fully curtailed at midday; Ember calls this a "notional loss to the system" because generators were compensated under TRAS — [Ember](https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/beyond-capacity-why-indias-power-system-must-get-flexible-to-harness-its-solar-potential/2025-saw-significant-solar-curtailment-to-preserve/); [PV Magazine 28 Jan 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/01/28/india-curtailed-2-3-twh-of-solar-between-may-and-december-2025-2/); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/india-curtails-23-twh-of-solar-power-in-2025-as-grid-flexibility-falls-short-ember-report). Note: Ember's "compensated" framing conflicts with the Rajasthan industry's INR 250 crore loss claim; the latter likely reflects transmission-constraint curtailment (not TRAS-compensated) — flagged as a conflict.
- Causes: coal plants held at 55% minimum technical load; transmission delays (Khetri–Narela line added only ~600 MW effective margin per SPDA); in Q1 2026 transmission constraints were ~two-thirds of all RE curtailment — [Ember](https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/beyond-capacity-why-indias-power-system-must-get-flexible-to-harness-its-solar-potential/structural-and-operational-factors-of-renewable-en); [Ember transmission note](https://ember-energy.org/?p=24081); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/curtailment-transmission-bottlenecks-and-storage-gaps-dominate-indias-power-transition-outlook)
- JMK (Grid-India data): 96.9 GW of solar and 21.7 GW of wind capacity "affected by curtailment" in Sep 2026; Rajasthan 110 MU curtailed in Sep 2026, all solar — [JMK Research](https://jmkresearch.com/96-9-gw-of-solar-and-21-7-gw-of-wind-capacity-affected-by-curtailment-in-september-2026/)

**Exchange price capture (merchant exposure)**
- IEX solar capture rate fell from ~INR 5/kWh in FY23 to INR 2.23/kWh in FY26 because prices are near zero at peak solar hours — [PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/is-standalone-solar-losing-its-edge-in-india/)
- Ember: average annual capture rate on IEX DAM (for a flat/average generator) dropped from 93% to 70% between 2019 and 2024 — [Ember, Age of Storage](https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/the-age-of-storage-batteries-primed-for-indias-power-markets/chapter-3-the-economics-of-merchant-bess)
- FY26 IEX averages: DAM INR 3.86/kWh (−13.7% YoY), RTM INR 3.59/kWh (−16% YoY), G-DAM INR 3.59/kWh (−10.6% YoY) — [SolarQuarter](https://solarquarter.com/2026/04/06/indian-energy-exchange-reports-record-141-bu-trade-volume-in-fy26-prices-decline-on-higher-supply/); [Mercom](https://mercomindia.com/iex-trades-record-electricity-of-141-bu-in-fy-2026-breaks-last-years-record)
- Vendor claim (benchmark, not India-specific): Fluence Mosaic claims revenue uplift of up to 10% for renewables and up to 50% for storage — [Fluence](https://fluenceenergy.com/energy-bidding-software/)

### Inferences
- Illustrative 100 MW solar (assume ~190 MU/yr at ~22% CUF; CUF assumption is mine, not sourced): DSM at INR 0.04-0.05/kWh = INR 0.76-0.95 crore/yr pre-April 2026. 100 MW wind (~260 MU/yr at ~30% CUF): INR 0.07-0.08/kWh = INR 1.8-2.1 crore/yr, consistent with the 2.8%-of-revenue example. Applying IWPA's +35-40% for the band tightening gives ~INR 2.5-2.9 crore/yr for wind from FY27, before the X-factor glide path compounds it.
- Software's recoverable share of DSM: the penalty-free band is now ±5%/±10%, and NGEL's data show ~40% of wind blocks already outside ±5%; the economic lever is reducing the frequency and size of out-of-band blocks via better day-ahead/intraday forecasts and the 16-revisions-a-day scheduling. A plausible recovery range is 20-50% of DSM charges (i.e. INR 0.2-0.5 crore/yr solar, INR 0.5-1.4 crore/yr wind for 100 MW); this is my estimate — no Indian vendor publishes verified before/after numbers.
- Curtailment loss for a Rajasthan-connected 100 MW solar plant: INR 250 crore / 4 GW over ~6 months ≈ INR 6 crore per 100 MW per half-year (~INR 12 crore annualised at 2025 severity). Software cannot recover transmission-constraint curtailment but can (a) time maintenance into curtailment windows, (b) exploit TRAS compensation rules, and (c) co-optimise with storage.
- Merchant bidding: the gap between FY26 DAM average (3.86) and solar capture (2.23) is INR 1.63/kWh ≈ INR 31 crore/yr on 190 MU — but it is structural (midday glut), not a bidding error. Software recovery for pure solar is limited to market-selection (DAM vs RTM vs G-DAM vs G-TAM, 27 paise/kWh FY26 DAM-RTM gap ≈ INR 5 crore/yr upper bound if one always picked the better venue) and avoidance of zero-price blocks. For storage-backed plants the May 2026 trough-to-peak spread of INR 8.60/kWh (see Q3) is the real prize.

### Gaps
- No published annual total of DSM charges paid by Indian wind/solar generators (Grid-India publishes weekly entity-level accounts only; Prayas has the 15-minute dataset but the rupee totals were not in the extract I could read).
- No Indian vendor publishes audited "DSM saved per MW" figures; all recovery estimates are inference.
- Could not fetch the full PV Magazine India (Jun 2026) article or the CERC X-factor order to confirm the wind glide path beyond FY30.

## Key Question 2: What do generators pay today for forecasting/scheduling (QCA) and trading services? Who are the incumbents and how sticky are they?

### Takeaway
Forecasting & scheduling in India is a commoditised, association-negotiated service priced at roughly INR 4,000-4,200/MW/year plus GST (~INR 330-350/MW/month) for wind under IWPA group contracts, with state QCAs also posting corpus/security deposits of INR 10,000-50,000/MW; the market leader by revenue, REConnect, made only INR 52 crore in FY25 across ~12-22 GW, confirming that pure F&S is a tiny (~INR 100 crore/yr) market and that value must come from DSM savings, trading and portfolio optimisation layered on top.

### Cited Findings

**Pricing**

### Inferences
- Pure F&S TAM: at INR ~4,000/MW/yr across India's ~165 GW solar (Jun 2026) plus ~55 GW wind, the entire F&S fee pool is on the order of INR 80-100 crore/yr — consistent with REConnect (INR 52 crore incl. C&I services) and Manikaran being the two largest. This is not a venture-scale market by itself.
- Stickiness is moderate: IWPA-style association contracts are re-tendered annually (FY-wise circulars), suggesting low switching cost on price; but QCAs carry SLDC registration, corpus deposits (INR 25-50k/MW in Maharashtra), metering/telemetry integration and commercial settlement responsibility, which create operational lock-in.
- Pricing power will shift to whoever can demonstrably cut DSM under the ±5%/±10% bands and the X-factor glide: a 20-50% DSM reduction on wind (INR 0.5-1.4 crore/yr per 100 MW, Q1 inference) is 12-35x the current F&S fee of ~INR 4 lakh/yr per 100 MW, so outcome-linked pricing (share of DSM saved) is economically feasible.

## Key Question 3: How are merchant renewable and storage-backed plants monetising exchange volatility in 2025-2026? Typical DAM vs RTM spreads and midday troughs?

### Takeaway
IEX has bifurcated into a near-zero midday market and a ceiling-bound evening market: the May midday trough fell from INR 2.81/kWh (2023) to INR 1.11/kWh (2026) while the evening peak rose from INR 8.08 to INR 9.71 against a INR 10 cap, an intraday spread of INR 8.60/kWh; merchant standalone solar is being squeezed (capture INR 2.23/kWh FY26) while merchant BESS became profitable for the first time in 2024 and the first 100 MWh merchant battery reached full operation in Jan 2026.

TAM (incl. day-ahead contingency) 12.72 BU (+8%); Green market (G-DAM + G-TAM) 10.78 BU; G-DAM Q4 FY26 2.44 BU (+26.5%). ## Key Question 4: What does market coupling (CERC order 2025) change for traders and optimisers?

### Takeaway
CERC's 23 Jul 2025 suo motu order (Petition 8/SM/2025) directed phased coupling starting with DAM by Jan 2026, but the original exchange round-robin design was dropped in favour of Grid-India as MCO (draft Power Market Second Amendment Regulations, 21 Apr 2026); as of the latest sources found, DAM coupling had not gone live. Grid-India's own shadow pilot found only ~INR 38 crore (0.3%) welfare gain and +52 MU (0.2%) volume, so the practical effect is one uniform DAM price, lower exchange fee competition risk for IEX (~90% share), and removal of cross-exchange arbitrage rather than a change in generator economics.

### Cited Findings
- CERC suo motu order of 23 Jul 2025 (Petition 8/SM/2025) started phased coupling with DAM by Jan 2026 under a round-robin MCO (IEX, PXIL, HPX rotating; Grid-India as backup/audit operator); it followed Petition 1/SM/2024 (Feb 2024) — [SolarQuarter](https://solarquarter.com/2025/07/24/cerc-directs-phased-implementation-of-market-coupling-in-power-exchanges-starting-january-2026/); [Mercom](https://mercomindia.com/cerc-directs-phased-rollout-of-power-market-coupling-from-january-2026); [Powerline Sep 2025](https://powerline.net.in/2025/09/01/unifying-the-grid-market-coupling-to-reshape-power-trading/)

### Inferences
- For a generator optimiser, coupling removes the (small) venue-arbitrage question across IEX/PXIL/HPX for DAM and makes one algorithm (Grid-India's) the price-setter — this simplifies bidding logic and makes price-forecast models exchange-agnostic. It does not touch RTM, G-DAM, TAM or bilateral venues yet, where most RE optimisation value sits.
- Coupling plus fee rationalisation may lower IEX's transaction fee moat and encourage exchanges to compete on member tools/APIs — a potential distribution channel for third-party optimisers.
- Data implication: a single MCO publishing a national clearing algorithm and audited results should improve transparency of aggregate bid/offer curves, though pre-clearing bid data remains excluded from the draft National Electricity Data Sharing Framework (see Q6).

## Key Question 6: What data access and API realities exist in India (SLDC/RLDC, Grid-India, IEX, plant SCADA)?

### Takeaway
There is no public developer API for RLDC/SLDC real-time data or for IEX order entry; generators are obligated to push turbine/inverter-level availability and pooling-station generation to the grid operator, RLDC forecasts are posted on regional websites, and the June 2026 draft National Electricity Data Sharing Framework proposes a National Electricity Data Portal (via API Setu/NAPIX) while explicitly excluding pre-clearing exchange bid data.

### Cited Findings
- Grid-India procedure: RE generators must provide real-time availability at turbine/inverter level and generation data at pooling-station level; RLDC forecasts are made available on the concerned RLDC's website — [Grid-India procedure](https://grid-india.in/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/pro.pdf); [Grid-India data collection procedure, First Amendment 2023](https://grid-india.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Procedure-for-collection-of-data-and-information-First-Amendment_Final.pdf)
- A 2025 MNRE/central solar data platform document says vendors access a web API with unique credentials (solar data exchange, not grid-operator feeds) — [MNRE PDF](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3716e1b8c6cd17b771da77391355749f3/uploads/2025/07/202507231586051100.pdf)
- Draft National Electricity Data Sharing Framework (22 Jun 2026): defines "Data Requestor", proposes a National Electricity Data Portal and references API Setu and NAPIX; "power exchange bid data prior to market clearing shall remain outside the scope of sharing" — [Draft framework PDF](https://images.assettype.com/downtoearth/2026-07-02/vyxv2grj/draft_National_Electricity_Data_Sharing_Framework_2026__June_22_2026.pdf)
- CEA publishes an API for installed-capacity data; CEA+CEEW India Renewables Dashboard allows multi-format downloads; CEA monthly "dashboard reports" cover exchange prices — [CEA API](https://cea.nic.in/api-for-central-electricity-authority-data/?lang=en); [PV Magazine India on RE portal](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2021/04/22/one-stop-portal-launched-to-provide-all-india-renewable-energy-generation-data); [Prayas RE data portal launch](https://energy.prayaspune.org/our-work/webinar/launch-of-the-india-re-data-portal)
- Research datasets: hourly all-India demand, solar and wind generation Sep 2021–Jun 2025 (Mendeley); CEA+Grid-India operational data Oct 2025–Oct 2026 (IEEE DataPort, Parquet/CSV) — [Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/y58jknpgs8); [IEEE DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/keywords/electricity-supply-and-demand)
- CERC Market Monitoring Cell publishes monthly short-term market reports (volumes, prices by segment) — [CERC MMC Mar 2025](https://cercind.gov.in/2025/market_monitoring/MMC%20Report%20on%20Short%20term%20market%20for%20March%202025.pdf)
- T-GNA and green open access are processed through Grid-India's NOAR portal — [NLDC T-GNA via NOAR](https://posoco.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/NLDC_Procedure-for-Grant-of-T-GNA-through-NOAR.pdf); [Green OA procedure Rev 4, Jul 2024](https://devancillary.grid-india.in/assets/files/Procedure%20of%20Green%20Energy%20Open%20Access%20Revision%204_08%20Jul%202024.pdf)
- Grid-India/POSOCO ran forecasting training workshops (2024) describing REMC data flows — [POSOCO solar forecasting session](https://posoco.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Solar_Power_Forecasting_Session_3_Day_2_compressed.pdf); [POSOCO wind forecasting session](https://posoco.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Wind_Energy_Forecasting_Session_3_Day1_compressed.pdf)
- Trading licensees must file monthly Form IV returns with CERC (example: Tata Power Trading, ReNew Energy Markets) — [TPTCL Form IV Aug 2025 DAM](https://www.tatapowertrading.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/TRF4-F-Aug-25-DAM.pdf); [ReNew Form IV](https://renew.com/resources/renew-india/renew-energy-markets-pvt-ltd/Form-IV-Feb-26.pdf)

### Inferences
- Practical data stack for an Indian optimiser today: (1) plant SCADA/inverter data via the generator's own OEM/monitoring systems (Prescinto-type integrations); (2) SLDC/RLDC schedule, DSM and curtailment data obtained as a registered QCA/generator, not via public API; (3) IEX historical MCP/MCV via website downloads and CERC/CEA monthly reports; (4) bids placed through IEX member terminals (traders or direct members) — no evidence of a public bidding API. Partnership with a trading licensee is therefore the realistic execution path.
- The 2026 draft data framework, if notified, would lower data-acquisition cost for price/load forecasting but keeps the most valuable (pre-clearing bid stack) data closed.

### Gaps
- No documentation found on IEX member API/algorithmic bidding capabilities (search budget exhausted before this query ran).
- Status of the National Electricity Data Sharing Framework after June 2026 unknown.
- No source on SCADA-access practices between IPPs and third-party optimisers in India (NDAs/OEM gateways).
