# AuriHire business model and value share with Summit Executive Recruiter

Internal working document (2026-11-15). Negotiation positions are included, so this is not the version to send to Summit. Figures marked *placeholder* are starting points to validate against Summit's numbers, not recommendations.

## 1. What Summit actually is to AuriHire

Summit is three counterparties in one firm. Price each separately, or every conversation collapses into "give us a discount".

| Role | What they give | What they want | Pay them in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer | Live Director-plus searches run through AuriHire; a US reference logo | Faster shortlists, defensible rejections, a differentiator in pitches | Product, at a design-partner price |
| SME | Recruiter time on the plan-review step; edits to criteria and tiers; a life-sciences criteria library | Influence over the product; credit; a say in the pharma vertical | Credits and a bounded vertical revenue share |
| Sales channel | Introductions to their clients' in-house talent teams; co-selling into life sciences | A cut of revenue they create | Referral or co-sell commission on cash collected |

The sales channel is narrower than it sounds. Summit selling AuriHire to other search firms means arming its competitors, and they will not do it well. Their real distribution is the corporate talent teams at the pharma and biotech clients they already place executives into. That is the account list to build the referral programme around.

## 2. AuriHire's own pricing model

The product's economics: a planner call per job, an extractor call per resume (stored and reused for every later job, so marginal cost per resume falls as the pool grows), a screen call per resume per job, and a second-reader call per critical rejection. Cost scales with resumes screened; value scales with searches closed.

Recommendation: **per-seat subscription with an active-job allowance**, not per-resume usage.

- Per-resume pricing punishes exactly the behaviour the product wants (screen the whole pool, not a sample) and makes the invoice unpredictable.
- Per-search pricing is intuitive for an agency but invites gaming (one "search" with five JDs) and undercharges in-house teams that run many roles.
- Per seat with an allowance tracks headcount, which is how both agencies and in-house teams already budget.

Three tiers, *placeholder* price points for the US:

| Tier | Who | Price | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agency | Search firms | $350 per recruiter seat per month | 10 active jobs per seat, fair-use resume cap per job, second reader on every critical rejection |
| Talent team | In-house TA | $250 per seat per month, minimum 5 seats | Same, plus audit export for AI-hiring compliance |
| Enterprise | Pharma TA at scale | Annual contract, custom | Shared dictionary tuned to their taxonomy, SSO, data residency |

Sanity check against the buyer's economics: a retained search for a $300k Director carries a fee of roughly $75k to $100k. A seat at $350 a month is under 5% of one search fee per year. The price is not the objection; proof that the screen is right is. That is why the design-partner phase comes before the pricing conversation.

India pricing is a separate sheet. Do not let the Summit numbers set the India list.

## 3. Value share, by role

### 3.1 Summit as customer: design partner, then paying customer

- 6 months (*placeholder*) at a design-partner rate: free for up to 5 seats, or 50% off list.
- In exchange: minimum 3 live searches run end to end in the product, a written case study, a reference call per quarter, and the SME hours below.
- Conversion clause: at month 6 they move to the Agency tier at list less a permanent 20% founding-partner discount. The discount is tied to staying a reference customer.
- No perpetual free use. Free forever makes their feedback worthless as pricing signal and makes the next partner demand the same.

### 3.2 Summit as SME: credits, not cash, plus a bounded vertical share

What their SME work is worth to AuriHire: the plan-review edits and screen overrides are the ground truth that the shared dictionary, the tier rules and the eval suite are missing. Three months of real Director-plus pharma searches is more signal than any amount of synthetic JDs.

Structure:

- **Credits.** Each hour of logged SME review (plan edits, screen overrides with reasons, dictionary corrections) earns a credit against their subscription, at a rate that makes 10 hours a month roughly cover one seat (*placeholder*: $35 per hour). Capped at 100% of their own bill, so SME work can zero their invoice but never becomes a cash obligation for AuriHire.
- **Vertical revenue share.** If Summit's SMEs substantively author a life-sciences criteria library or plan templates that AuriHire sells to third parties as a vertical pack, Summit receives 10% of net revenue from that pack for 24 months after launch. "Substantively" is defined in writing: a named deliverable, accepted by AuriHire, not general feedback.
- **IP.** AuriHire owns the dictionary, templates, prompts and evals outright. Summit gets a perpetual, non-exclusive, royalty-free licence to use what it helped build inside AuriHire, and attribution as a vertical partner. Nothing Summit contributes can be withdrawn later; nothing AuriHire builds becomes Summit's.
- **Data.** Summit's SMEs see only Summit's own jobs and candidates. Multi-tenancy is a precondition of the SME role, not a feature request from it. Logged edits used for product learning are anonymised at the job level before they leave Summit's tenant.

### 3.3 Summit as sales channel: three tiers of involvement

Pay on cash collected, quarterly in arrears, net of refunds and credits. Never pay on bookings.

| Tier | Summit does | AuriHire does | Summit gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Referral | Warm introduction, deal registered | Demo, proposal, contract, onboarding, support | 15% of year-1 cash from that customer |
| Co-sell | Introduction plus runs the first demo and sponsors internally | Proposal, contract, onboarding, support | 20% of year-1 cash, 10% of year-2 cash |
| Resell | Contracts, bills and supports the customer under Summit's paper | Product, second-line support, compliance documentation | 30% off list; sets price above a floor of 80% of list |

Mechanics that stop the usual fights:

- Deal registration with 90-day protection. A registered account that AuriHire later closes direct within the window still pays the referral rate.
- "Sourced" means the first qualified meeting came through Summit. "Influenced" pays nothing; it is the clause every channel conflict comes from.
- Clawback: if the customer churns or defaults inside the first 6 months, the commission on uncollected cash is not owed and paid commission on refunded cash is netted from the next payout.
- Resell only when Summit has closed at least three referral or co-sell deals. Reseller margins are for partners who have proved they can sell, not a signing bonus.

### 3.4 Equity: not now

A strategic partner will raise it. The honest answer is that advisor equity for a channel partner is almost always regretted: it rewards one early partner for a relationship that commission already pays for, and it complicates the next partner and the next round. If pressed, offer warrants that vest only against a revenue milestone (for example, $250k of partner-sourced annual recurring revenue), expiring in 24 months. That converts the ask into a sales target.

The point of the table is the shape, not the totals: Summit's own subscription is the smallest line, and the channel is where both sides make money. Price the design partnership to get into the channel, not to extract revenue from Summit directly.

## 5. Terms to protect

1. **Exclusivity.** Default none. If Summit insists, offer US life-sciences exclusivity on the referral programme for 12 months, conditional on a quota (*placeholder*: 3 closed deals in the first 6 months), lapsing automatically if missed. Never exclusivity on the product or the vertical.
2. **Non-compete on the tool.** Summit may not build, fund or resell a competing screening product for the term plus 12 months. They will ask the same of AuriHire regarding search services; agree to it, AuriHire is not a search firm.
3. **Compliance allocation.** US customers face EEOC rules and state and city AI-hiring laws (NYC Local Law 144 bias audits and notices; Colorado and Illinois AI acts). AuriHire supplies the audit export, the second-reader records and the protected-attribute controls; the customer deploys the notices and decides on candidates. Write this allocation into the reseller paper too, or Summit inherits the liability and will price for it.
4. **Brand use.** Each side may name the other as a partner; neither may imply endorsement of a client-specific outcome.
5. **Term and exit.** 12 months, auto-renew, 60 days' notice. Commissions on customers closed before termination keep paying for their scheduled period.
6. **Most-favoured-nation.** Decline. Partner terms will change as the programme matures.

| 2. Co-sell or resell | 9+ | Co-sell on enterprise accounts; resell if Summit wants to own the paper | 3 closed deals, support process tested |

## 7. Decisions needed from Manoj before the next conversation with Summit

1. The three price points, or at least the order of magnitude. Everything in §3 is a percentage of them.
2. Whether Summit may ever sell to other search firms. The model above says no; if yes, the referral rate for that segment should be lower (10%) because those deals will need more AuriHire effort.
3. Whether AuriHire is willing to offer any exclusivity at all.
4. Whether the vertical revenue share in §3.2 is acceptable, or whether SME work is credits only.
5. The equity line: hold the "not now" position, or pre-decide a milestone warrant.
6. Multi-tenancy readiness: if Summit's SMEs cannot be isolated to Summit's data today, the SME role waits until they can.
