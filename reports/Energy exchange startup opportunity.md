# Sell Flexibility to Generators Before Households

There is a viable startup opportunity in India's power market, but it is narrower and more B2B than the "energy exchange for everyone" framing suggests, and the right order of plays is the reverse of the one most founders instinctively pick. The residential VPP (Play 1) is the weakest near-term bet: India has roughly 1 GWh of installed batteries across all segments, no national aggregator licence, smart meters at about 29% of the sanctioned rollout, and a benchmark payout to a battery household that clusters at USD 130-300 a year in the US and Australia and about GBP 5 a season in the UK, which for a 3 kW subsidised rooftop household translates to low hundreds of rupees of flexibility value. Generator intelligence (Play 2) has a tiny fee pool today, roughly INR 80-100 crore a year for forecasting and scheduling, but sits on top of a penalty pool that regulation is deliberately enlarging: from April 2026 deviation bands for solar halved to ±5% and for wind tightened to ±10%, the wind industry itself estimates a 35-40% jump in deviation charges, and the X-factor glide path makes schedule deviations fully chargeable by 2031. Aggregated storage (Play 3) is where the money is largest, with IEX intraday spreads reaching INR 8.60/kWh in May 2026 and modelled merchant battery IRRs of 15-22%, but every mature market shows that window closing within three to five years of fleet build-out, and most contracted Indian BESS capacity is locked into 12-year availability contracts that an aggregator cannot touch. The recommendation is to start with Play 2 as an outcome-priced DSM-reduction and dispatch service for wind, solar and hybrid generators, delivered through a trader partner rather than a licence of one's own, and to use the resulting data, SLDC/RLDC integrations and generator relationships as the wedge into Play 3, becoming India's first independent floor-and-share optimiser for merchant and co-located batteries as the 2026-28 build-out lands. Play 1 should be deferred until a home-battery subsidy (the mooted PM Surya Ghar 2.0) and a notified demand-response framework exist, and even then pursued as software sold to DISCOMs and installers, not as a consumer app, because every standalone residential aggregator in the benchmark markets either folded or was absorbed.

## India's exchange is turning into a shape market, and that is the whole opportunity

The headline numbers describe a market growing in volume while collapsing in price. IEX traded a record **141 BU in FY26 (+17%)**, with the day-ahead market flat at 62.78 BU and the real-time market the growth engine; the average DAM price fell to **INR 3.86/kWh (-13.7%)** and RTM to INR 3.59 ([Energetica India](https://energetica-india.net/news/iex-reports-141-bu-fy26-volume-rec-trade-surges-119-9-percent-yoy-in-march)). All three exchanges together traded 168.55 BU, with IEX at 84%, PXIL 11% and HPX 5% ([Indian Infrastructure](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2026/07/03/trading-trends-shift-to-market-based-solutions/)). Two years earlier the FY24 DAM average had been INR 5.24 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/iex-reports-19-rise-in-trade-volume-at-121-billion-units-in-fy25-125040400578_1.html)), so prices are down roughly 27% while IEX volume is up roughly 40%, a combination that tells any founder the money in this market is no longer in energy, it is in time.

The intraday shape confirms this. An EAC-PM working paper tracks the May midday trough falling from **INR 2.81/kWh in 2023 to INR 1.11 in 2026** while the evening peak rose from INR 8.08 to INR 9.71 against a INR 10 cap, taking the trough-to-peak spread from INR 5.27 to **INR 8.60/kWh in three years** ([EAC-PM](https://eacpm.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026.07.03_Working_Paper_Current.pdf)). On 10 April 2026 the DAM cleared at INR 399/MWh at noon and INR 10,000/MWh at 19:00 ([BigMint](https://www.bigmint.co/insights/detail/india-faces-power-glut-by-day-shortage-by-night-as-demand-grows-746957)). Standalone solar is the casualty: the IEX solar capture rate fell from about **INR 5/kWh in FY23 to INR 2.23/kWh in FY26** ([PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/is-standalone-solar-losing-its-edge-in-india/)), and Ember finds the capture rate for a flat generator on the DAM dropped from 93% to 70% between 2019 and 2024 ([Ember](https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/the-age-of-storage-batteries-primed-for-indias-power-markets/chapter-3-the-economics-of-merchant-bess)). Curtailment adds a physical layer to the price signal. Ember counts about **2.3 TWh of solar curtailed between May and December 2025**, roughly 40% of it in October alone ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/01/28/india-curtailed-2-3-twh-of-solar-between-may-and-december-2025-2/)), and about 470 GWh of inter-state RE curtailment in Q1 2026, two-thirds of it transmission-driven and concentrated in Rajasthan and Gujarat ([Ember](https://ember-energy.org/latest-updates/india-lost-300-million-units-of-renewable-energy-owing-to-transmission-constraints-in-q1-2026/)). The notes disagree on who bore the cost: Ember describes the TRAS-instructed curtailment as a "notional loss to the system" because generators were compensated, whereas Rajasthan developers reported losses of up to **INR 250 crore across 3-4 GW** between March and August 2025 as curtailment shares rose from 8.5% to 51.5% ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/rajasthan-curtails-4-gw-of-solar-capacity-since-march-industry-incurs-losses-of-up-to-rs-250-crore)). The most plausible reconciliation, which the notes flag as inference, is that TRAS-dispatched curtailment is compensated while transmission-constraint curtailment is not; a founder selling into Rajasthan should verify which regime a prospective client sits under before promising recovery.

Regulation is tightening in the direction that rewards precision. The CERC DSM Regulations 2024 narrowed the penalty-free deviation band from **±10% to ±5% for solar and hybrid and ±15% to ±10% for wind from 1 April 2026** ([Energetica India](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/cerc-notifies-phased-x-factor-reduction-for-wind-and-solar-tightens-deviation-bands-from-april-2026)), and a 31 March 2026 order set the X-factor glide path under which the share of available capacity in the deviation denominator falls to 55% for solar and 65% for wind by FY30 and to zero, meaning pure schedule-based settlement, by April 2031 ([JSA](https://www.jsalaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/JSA-Prism-ElectricityPower-April-2026-CERC-Order.Final_.pdf)). The surrounding reform calendar is almost entirely at draft stage: market coupling, ordered in July 2025, had no notified regulations as of IEX's 30 October 2026 earnings call, and management expects another 1.5-2 years after notification ([Multibagg](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/iex-market-coupling-timeline-concall-cmw3izytl00032kqqyvbvswu2)); Grid-India's own shadow pilot found only a ~INR 38 crore (0.3%) welfare gain from DAM coupling ([Anantam summary](https://anantamias.com/current-affairs/market-coupling-in-dam-segment-of-power-exchanges-may-have-little-benefit/)), so for an optimiser coupling simplifies price modelling rather than changing economics. The draft National Electricity Policy 2026 directs commissions to "introduce aggregators" for prosumers, storage and EVs with no charges beyond network costs ([RAP](https://www.raponline.org/knowledge-center/comments-to-indias-ministry-of-power-on-the-draft-national-electricity-policy-2026/)); CERC floated a 5-minute RTM discussion paper in May 2026 ([Power Line](https://powerline.net.in/2026/05/08/cerc-releases-discussion-paper-proposing-shorter-scheduling-timeline-for-rtm/)) and a non-binding capacity-market staff paper in April 2026 ([Power Line](https://powerline.net.in/2026/05/06/cerc-issues-staff-paper-on-capacity-market-for-electricity-in-india/)); cash-settled electricity futures launched on MCX and NSE in July 2025 ([Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/updates/mcx-launches-electricity-futures-elecdmbl)). The pattern matters for sequencing: the instruments that are already binding (DSM bands, X-factor, solar/non-solar GNA split, ToD rules) all penalise or reward generators and storage owners, while the instruments that would create a residential flexibility market (aggregator status, demand-response frameworks, 5-minute settlement) are all still drafts.

## Play 1: the residential VPP is a 2029 business in India, not a 2027 one

The benchmark markets are brutally consistent about what a household actually earns. Sunrun's CalReady programme paid customers up to USD 150 per battery and about USD 10 million in total across roughly 75,000 batteries in 2025 ([Sunrun](https://investors.sunrun.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/340/sunruns-distributed-power-plant-quadruples-in-size-to)), which the notes compute as roughly **USD 133 per battery per year**. SEIA puts the defensible range for a full event season at USD 200-300/kW ([SEIA](https://seia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/VPP-Factsheet_Dec-2025.pdf)), with the USD 800-1,000 figures confined to capacity-constrained New England programmes and sourced only from secondary trackers. Octopus paid more than 2 million UK households over GBP 10 million in winter 2024/25, about **GBP 5 a household** ([Octopus Energy](https://octopus.energy/press/pay-people-not-power-plants/)), and NESO's Demand Flexibility Service collapsed from GBP 11.9 million of payouts in 2023/24 to about GBP 1 million once the subsidised guaranteed price ended ([NESO](https://www.neso.energy/nearly-2-million-households-and-businesses-registered-demand-flexibility-service-dfs-last-winter)). In Australia, VPP households received a median of about AUD 55 a quarter per ACCC/ECA data cited by Amber ([Amber](https://www.amber.com.au/blog/battery-owners-arent-sold-on-vpps-it-comes-down-to-control-and-money)), AGL's programme caps event credits at about AUD 250 a year ([Gridly](https://www.gridly.com.au/blog/agl-vpp-review-australia)), and IEEFA found VPP revenue small relative to the household's own self-consumption savings, with two system events supplying 49% of all FCAS revenue in AEMO's demonstrations ([IEEFA](https://ieefa.org/resources/virtual-power-plants-are-leveraging-australian-consumer-investment-rooftop-solar)). Germany's value arrives mostly as dynamic-tariff bill savings; an HTW Berlin study measured effects as small as EUR 35 a year from a battery-dispatch tweak against vendor claims of EUR 620 ([HTW Berlin](https://solar.htw-berlin.de/wp-content/uploads/MEISSNER-2026-Impact-of-dynamic-electricity-tariffs-and-time-of-use-grid-charges-on-households-with-pv-bess.pdf)). Even where batteries are subsidised, enrolment lags: Australia's Cheaper Home Batteries Program had passed 400,000 installs by May 2026 with "barely a VPP in sight" ([Energy Magazine](https://www.energymagazine.com.au/400000-home-batteries-installed-but-barely-a-vpp-in-sight/)).

The failure record points at the business model rather than the technology. Swell Energy, which raised more than USD 100 million to finance batteries against future programme revenue, liquidated in August 2024, with analysts citing sector immaturity, thin margins and the cost of winning customers one at a time ([Latitude Media](https://latitudemedia.com/news/scoop-swell-is-shutting-down)). OhmConnect's standalone behavioural-DR model survived only by merging into Google's Nest Renew ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/google-nest-renew-ohmconnect-combine-vpp/715616/)). Shell bought sonnen for about EUR 500 million in 2019 and sold it in August 2026 at a reported loss of about EUR 1 billion ([ESS News](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/08/28/shell-sells-sonnen-to-tiven/)). Behavioural programmes report 12% participant churn driven by comfort and loss-of-control concerns ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/demand-response-dr-utility-programs-resideo/754205/)). The models that scaled own the customer or the asset: Kraken, spun out at a reported **USD 8.65 billion** on licensed utility software ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/30/octopus-energy-to-spinoff-ai-unit-kraken-at-8point65-billion-valuation.html)); Base Power, which owns the home batteries and sells the retail plan, at USD 4 billion after a USD 1 billion Series C ([ESS News](https://www.ess-news.com/2025/10/09/base-power-hauls-in-1-billion-to-take-its-distributed-home-battery-model-beyond-texas/)); and third-party-owned solar-plus-storage, projected to reach 64% of US residential installs in 2026, where the owner enrols the battery by default ([EnergyHub](https://www.energyhub.com/news/unlocking-battery-vpp-scale-how-third-party-owners-are-solving-for-growth)).

India has the demand-side raw material but none of the three enablers in finished form. PM Surya Ghar has put rooftop solar on **50 lakh households with 14.8 GW commissioned** by August 2026, an average of about 3 kW each ([Energetica India](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/pm-surya-ghar-scheme-crosses-50-lakh-rooftop-solar-installations-14-8-gw-capacity-commissioned)), and the Australian sequence of hosting caps, lower export tariffs and storage mandates is visibly starting: MERC's September 2026 draft would cap rooftop capacity at 70% of feeder rating and mandate storage above 100 kW ([SolarQuarter](https://solarquarter.com/2026/09/22/merc-proposes-new-rooftop-renewable-rules-with-mandatory-ess-and-70-grid-capacity-cap-in-maharashtra/)), MERC proposed INR 2.82/kWh for surplus exports in FY27 ([Mercom](https://mercomindia.com/maharashtra-proposes-₹2-82-kwh-tariff-for-surplus-rooftop-solar-power)), and twelve states have moved to net billing ([Saur Energy](https://www.saurenergy.com/solar-energy-news/12-states-switch-to-net-billing-for-rooftop-solar-ceew)). But the batteries to shift that export do not exist: India added 547 MWh of BESS across all segments in 2025 for a cumulative 1,082 MWh ([Mercom](https://mercomindia.com/indias-energy-storage-capacity-rose-26-yoy-in-2025-adding-547-mwh)), a figure that conflicts with CEA's 5.3 GWh and IESA's 8.5 GWh operational counts cited in the storage notes, and the PM Surya Ghar subsidy covers PV only, with a battery-inclusive "2.0" still undecided as of August 2026 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/govt-weighs-industry-proposals-for-next-phase-of-rooftop-solar-scheme-126080501770_1.html)). Metering is partial: **5.73 crore RDSS smart meters installed against 20.33 crore sanctioned** as of June 2026, with the scheme extended to March 2028 ([T&D India](https://www.tndindia.com/indias-smart-meter-population-at-7-24-crore-parliament/amp/)). Price signals exist on paper, with ToD in 23 states and a Maharashtra solar-hour rebate of INR 0.80/unit ([CEEW](https://www.ceew.in/blogs/how-can-electricity-tariff-reforms-empower-indian-households)), but Ember's state scorecard shows residential alignment is patchy, with Telangana's ToD not solar-aligned, Karnataka uptake muted and Uttarakhand seeking deferment ([Ember](https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/indian-states-electricity-transition-set-2026/dimension-3-market-enablers)).

The counterparty problem is the binding one. A secondary analysis finds no Indian statute uses the term "virtual power plant" and that every documented programme is DISCOM-run demand response ([Uplift Growth](https://upliftgrowth.in/systems-analysis/virtual-power-plants/)). IEX membership categories do not include households ([IEX](https://iexindia.com/membership)), CERC's ancillary services require a 1 MW minimum with SCADA telemetry ([TeamLease RegTech](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/16242/cerc-ancillary-services-regulations-2022)), and the state frameworks that do exist make the aggregator a DISCOM-registered entity rather than a market participant: Maharashtra's 2024 regulations impose a demand-flexibility portfolio obligation of 7% over five years ([Saur Energy](https://www.saurenergy.com/solar-energy-news/merc-new-regulation-sets-7-flexible-demand-target-for-next-five-years)), and Rajasthan's 2026 regulations set a DFPO rising from 0.25% to 2% by FY30 with an aggregator registration framework ([Energetica India](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/rerc-issues-dfdsm-regulations-2026-with-fy-29-30-targets-bans-diesel-gensets)). The March 2026 draft consumer-rights rules would push every SERC to define demand-response provider eligibility ([Power Line](https://powerline.net.in/2026/03/17/ministry-of-power-issues-draft-electricity-rights-of-consumers-amendment-rules-2026/)), but critics call it "a rule without an ecosystem" because it specifies no signalling or settlement mechanism ([Raj Pratap](https://sites.google.com/view/rajpratap/demand-response-a-rule-without-an-ecosystem)). The only scaled residential flexibility in India is Tata Power-DDL's AutoGrid-based programme, which reported about 101,000 enrolled customers and 560 MW of cumulative reduction in FY2024 per a secondary source ([Resolven](https://resolven.com/blog/virtual-power-plants-explained)), against its own 2024 deck showing 26,400 customers in FY2023 and a 1,05,000-customer target for FY2025 ([TPDDL](https://seforall.org/system/files/2024-05/TPDDL_DSM%20initatives%20in%20Tata%20Power%20Delhi%20Distribution%20Limited.pdf)); the AutoGrid software behind it now sits inside Uplight, of which Octopus is taking a majority stake ([Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/octopus-energy-is-taking-a-majority-stake-in-uplight/)). Competition for a residential play is therefore not other startups but DISCOMs buying foreign DERMS, and the capital in Indian residential energy has gone to installers, with SolarSquare raising USD 53 million at a USD 450-500 million valuation in June 2026 ([Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/business-news/solarsquare-raises-usd-53-million-in-b-capital-led-series-c-round)).

The verdict: at a INR 2.82/kWh export tariff against retail tariffs of roughly INR 6-8/kWh, the notes infer that the only value a HEMS can deliver a PM Surya Ghar household today is INR 3-5 per kWh shifted into self-consumption, and with no battery and no capacity payment that is a few hundred rupees a year. A residential VPP becomes investable in India when three things coincide: a home-battery subsidy, a notified aggregator or DR-provider status in at least two large states, and smart-metered, solar-aligned ToD in those states. Delhi, Maharashtra, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu are the candidates; none has all three yet.

## Play 2: generator intelligence has a small fee pool but a large and growing penalty pool

Forecasting and scheduling in India is a commoditised, association-negotiated service. The Indian Wind Power Association capped QCA charges at **INR 4,200/MW plus GST for FY2023-24** ([IWPA](https://www.windpro.org/circula/IWPA-Circular-No.%204-of-2023-24-Engagement-of-QCA-for-FY-2023-24.pdf)). The market leader by revenue, REConnect Energy, made **INR 52 crore in FY25** with INR 3.8 crore of profit ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/reconnect-energy/financials/)) and has raised only about USD 343,000 in its lifetime ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/62020-09)); Manikaran Analytics claims a 95 GW F&S portfolio, a figure the notes flag as likely cumulative registrations since it exceeds India's ISTS wind-plus-solar fleet ([SAARC Energy deck](https://www.saarcenergy.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Renewable-Energy-Scheduling-SAARC-Webinar-v2.pdf)). Applying the IWPA rate to roughly 165 GW of solar and 55 GW of wind, the notes infer a total F&S fee pool of about **INR 80-100 crore a year**, which is not a venture-scale market. The exit history confirms it: Climate Connect was absorbed by ReNew in 2020 ([Mercom](https://mercomindia.com/renew-power-acquires-climate-connect)) and Prescinto, with 16 GW under management, was folded into IBM Maximo in 2024 at an undisclosed price ([ESG Dive](https://www.esgdive.com/news/ibm-aqcuires-prescinto-renewable-energy-asset-management-maximo-saas-reporting/730181/)). Globally, pure forecasting SaaS stays small too: Amperon has raised about USD 30 million with revenue estimated at USD 1-10 million ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/amperon/)), while the businesses that scaled took market risk on other people's assets, such as Statkraft's German VPP of more than 10 GW across 1,000-plus generators ([Statkraft](https://www.statkraft.com/link/076f1abca98840d1a4b0275cc82c0ff1.aspx)), Shell's purchase of Next Kraftwerke at 8.45 GW ([Argus](https://www.argusmedia.com/es/news/2190436-shell-acquires-next-kraftwerke-to-expand-power-business)) and Habitat Energy's more than 3 GW of storage under management across ERCOT, GB and Australia ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260415643233/en/)).

What makes the Indian version interesting is that the penalty pool is many times the fee pool and regulators are deliberately enlarging it. Industry estimates put DSM charges at **INR 0.04-0.05/kWh for solar and INR 0.07-0.08/kWh for wind** under the old bands, with deviations beyond 15% costing INR 0.50-1.50/kWh ([Bridge to India](https://bridgetoindia.com/forecasting-regulations-a-tough-proposition-for-developers/)); a 2026 worked example shows a INR 2.80/kWh wind tariff losing 2.8% to deviation charges ([Energetica India](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/cerc-notifies-phased-x-factor-reduction-for-wind-and-solar-tightens-deviation-bands-from-april-2026)). IWPA calculated that the ±15% to ±10% tightening alone raises wind DSM charges by **35-40%** ([IWPA](https://cercind.gov.in/2025/draft_reg/comments-DSM24/15.%20IWPA.pdf)), and NGEL's Fatehgarh data show **39.7% of 15-minute blocks already beyond ±5%** and 19.6% beyond ±10% ([NGEL](https://cercind.gov.in/2025/draft_reg/comments-DSM24/25.%20NGEL.pdf)), while Tata Power notes DSM impact "reduces significantly" when generators pool at a station ([Tata Power](https://cercind.gov.in/2025/draft_reg/comments-DSM24/35.%20Tata%20Power.pdf)). The notes' own arithmetic, flagged as inference with an assumed 22% solar and 30% wind CUF, puts pre-2026 DSM at INR 0.8-1 crore a year for a 100 MW solar plant and INR 1.8-2.1 crore for 100 MW of wind, rising to INR 2.5-2.9 crore for wind from FY27 before the X-factor compounds it; a 20-50% reduction would be worth INR 0.5-1.4 crore a year per 100 MW of wind, which is 12-35 times the current INR 4 lakh F&S fee. No Indian vendor publishes audited before-and-after DSM figures, so that recovery range is a hypothesis to validate, not a fact.

The merchant and shaped-PPA segments add a second layer that only matters with storage or flexible assets. Adani Green, ReNew, ACME and Statkraft have put about INR 15,000 crore into merchant RE ([The Core](https://www.thecore.in/business/see-spot-run-how-solar-overcapacity-is-stinging-the-spot-power-market-836397)), but the INR 1.63/kWh gap between the FY26 DAM average and solar capture is structural midday glut, not a bidding error; the notes estimate the venue-selection upside for pure solar at no more than about INR 5 crore a year per 100 MW even if one always picked the better of DAM and RTM. Where software earns real money is shape: SECI's FDRE-RTC tenders demand 90% availability over six evening-to-morning hours and discovered INR 5.25/kWh ([Saur Energy](https://www.saurenergy.com/solar-energy-blog/the-battery-revolution-why-indias-fdre-and-rtc-projects-face-an-existential-crisis-10904692)), and CERC's September 2025 GNA amendment split transmission access into solar and non-solar hours ([JMK](https://jmkresearch.com/cerc-segregates-gna-allocation-into-solar-and-non-solar/)), making hourly availability rather than annual energy the unit of value. Vendor claims of up to 10% revenue uplift for renewables and 50% for storage from bid optimisation ([Fluence](https://fluenceenergy.com/energy-bidding-software/)) are benchmarks from liquid US markets and should not be imported uncritically.

Execution realities shape the go-to-market. There is no public API for RLDC/SLDC real-time data or IEX order entry; generators push inverter-level data to the grid operator as an obligation, bids go through member terminals, and the June 2026 draft National Electricity Data Sharing Framework explicitly keeps pre-clearing exchange bid data out of scope ([Draft framework](https://images.assettype.com/downtoearth/2026-07-02/vyxv2grj/draft_National_Electricity_Data_Sharing_Framework_2026__June_22_2026.pdf)). Trading on behalf of generators requires an inter-State trading licence, with a **Category V licence needing INR 2 crore of net worth** plus a current-ratio test ([CERC](https://cercind.gov.in/2020/orders/557-TD-2020.pdf)); the notes record a conflict on Category I, with one CERC order describing INR 100 crore ([CERC](https://cercind.gov.in/2020/orders/15-SM-2020.pdf)) and a scanned regulation copy giving INR 75 crore ([Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/509795817/154)), and CERC has rejected downgrade applications lacking audited balance sheets, so the net-worth tests are enforced. The lower-friction route is to act as a client of an existing trader-member under the Power Market Regulations 2021 ([LegitQuest](https://www.legitquest.com/act/central-electricity-regulatory-commission-power-market-regulations-2021/CBB3)); traders handled 18.8% of short-term volume in FY24 ([Power Line](https://powerline.net.in/2025/10/29/hitting-record-volumes-power-trading-trends-and-developments/)) and PTC alone traded 92.8 BU in FY26 with about 56% through exchanges ([Multibagg](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/ptc-fy26-94359)). Competition in bidding software is thin and unfunded: PrudenTech offers 96-block DAM/G-DAM forecasting and auto-bidding ([PrudenTech](https://www.prudentpower.in/)), EMA Solutions sells analytics and derivatives advisory ([EMA Solutions](https://powermarkets.in/)), GNA Energy launched a "GNAi" platform in 2026 ([Energetica India](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/gna-energy-launches-gnai-ai-powered-power-intelligence-platform-at-bes-2026)), and PTC partnered with N-SIDE for portfolio tools ([pv magazine India](https://pv-magazine-india.com/2023/03/14/ptc-india-partners-belgiums-n-side-on-predictive-solution-for-power-market)). The failure modes are commoditisation back to the INR 4,000/MW fee, in-housing by large IPPs (ReNew's job postings still list QCA appointment as a task, implying it uses third parties even after buying Climate Connect ([ReNew careers](https://careers.renew.com/job/Engineer-Forecasting-&-Scheduling/41099344))), and the risk that a well-funded trader bundles optimisation for free. Payment risk is lower than for DISCOM-facing businesses because the customer is a generator, and the LPS Rules have cut legacy DISCOM dues from INR 1.4 lakh crore to about INR 3,330 crore ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/economy-and-policy/discoms-legacy-dues-reduce-to-3300-cr-from-139-lakh-cr-in-4-years-power-min)), improving generators' own liquidity.

## Play 3: aggregated batteries face a 3-5 year merchant window and a contracted fleet locked away

India's storage pipeline is enormous on paper and tiny in operation. The national plan calls for **47 GW / 236 GWh of BESS by FY32** within 411 GWh of total storage ([Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/india-requires-74gw-411gwh-of-energy-storage-by-2032-according-to-national-electricity-plan/)), a CEA official put operational BESS at 5.3 GWh with 185 GWh under development ([Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/india-has-5-3gwh-of-operational-bess-with-185gwh-under-development-cea-official-says/)), and IESA counted 8.5 GWh by mid-2026 ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/07/india-energy-storage-gap-demand-flexibility-opportunity)); these conflict with Mercom's 1.08 GWh cumulative figure cited above, probably on definitional grounds, and none of the three was verifiable against a primary dataset. India awarded about **10.4 GW of standalone BESS in 2025**, mostly 2-hour, with H2 bids described as speculative ([pv magazine India](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2026/05/19/india-awards-10-4-gw-of-standalone-bess-capacity-in-2025-but-tariff-viability-remains-a-concern-ieefa-jmk-report/)). Tariffs fell to INR 1.48 lakh/MW/month for 2-hour and INR 2.85 lakh for 4-hour systems, and against IEEFA's INR 2.3 lakh viability benchmark **about 75% of 2-hour awards are "at risk"** ([IEEFA](https://ieefa.org/resources/viability-standalone-battery-energy-storage-tariffs-discovered-2025)). The notes' arithmetic puts those contracts at INR 17.8-22.7 lakh/MW/yr for 2-hour systems against INR 2.2-2.8 crore/MW of capex, a 12-16 year simple payback that the INR 18 lakh/MWh VGF ([pv magazine India](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2025/06/10/power-ministry-announces-inr-5400-crore-viability-gap-funding-to-support-30-gwh-bess-development)) trims by only one to two years; no VGF was disbursed in FY25 because no project reached financial closure ([Saur Energy](https://www.saurenergy.com/solar-energy-news/no-viability-gap-funding-released-for-bess-in-fy25-govt)). These bids clear only if BNEF's USD 70/kWh stationary pack price keeps falling ([Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/li-ion-battery-pack-prices-fell-8-since-last-year-despite-metals-prices-rising-bloombergnef-says/)).

The merchant case is where an aggregator earns its keep, and it is genuinely attractive today. Ember finds the average daily IEX DAM spread has exceeded battery LCOS since early 2024, that merchant BESS turned profitable for the first time in 2024, and that 1-2 hour merchant systems can earn **15-22% IRR** ([Ember](https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/the-age-of-storage-batteries-primed-for-indias-power-markets/chapter-3-the-economics-of-merchant-bess)); Juniper Green's 100 MWh project, billed as India's first merchant BESS, reached full operation in January 2026 ([Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/indias-first-merchant-bess-juniper-green-brings-100mwh-project-into-full-operation/)). IEX management calls merchant BESS "the next RTM", says nearly 80% of commissioned BESS operates merchant, and expects the opportunity to last only **three to five years** before the evening deficit narrows ([Saur Energy](https://www.saurenergy.com/solar-energy-news/5-ways-iex-tracks-the-renewables-shift-in-india-12222390)); the 80% figure is single-source. The notes' arithmetic puts gross arbitrage for a 2-hour, once-a-day battery at INR 36-50 lakh/MW/yr at INR 5.5-7.5/kWh spreads, roughly double the contracted 2-hour tariff before losses, charges and degradation. The benchmark markets show how fast that evaporates: ERCOT merchant revenue fell from **USD 193/kW-yr in 2023 to about USD 29 in 2025** with only three high-earning days ([Modo Energy](https://modoenergy.com/research/why-were-ercot-battery-revenues-so-low-in-2025-weather-energy-arbitrage-builodout)), Australian FCAS fell from AUD 384k/MW/yr in 2020 to AUD 11k and now supplies 9% of battery revenue ([Modo Energy](https://modoenergy.com/research/fcas-saturation-battery-energy-storage-revenues-australia-nem)), CAISO hit a record-low USD 1.2/kW-month in December 2025 ([Modo Energy](https://modoenergy.com/research/me-bess-caiso-battery-revenues-lowest-ever-revenues-energy-arbitrage)), and GB fell to GBP 47k/MW/yr in December 2025 before recovering to GBP 93-99k in autumn 2026 ([Modo Energy](https://modoenergy.com/research/gb-bess-revenues-october-2026-gas-prices-spreads-balancing-mechanism-mdo-mdb)). Germany is the outlier at EUR 149-294k/MW/yr but is forecast to halve by 2030 ([Modo Energy](https://modoenergy.com/research/germany-battery-investment-outlook-q2-2026-executive-summary)). The lesson is that the owners who kept earning migrated from merchant exposure to floors and tolls: Gresham House is replacing a 568 MW Octopus toll with 789 MW of floor deals ([Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/uk-gresham-house-signs-789mw-floor-deals-octopus-toll-to-expire/)), European standalone tolling deals rose from 3 in 2024 to 15 in 2025 ([Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/merchant-keep-most-upside-europe-move-to-bess-tolling-agreements-in-focus/)), and floors with profit-sharing are favoured by lenders because they secure debt but not equity returns ([Modo Energy](https://modoenergy.com/research/gb-battery-energy-storage-derisking-returns-revenues-great-britain-offtake-ldes-p462-q3-2025)).

For an asset-light aggregator the regulatory picture is half-built. Storage is now delicensed and may be owned or operated by any standalone developer under the amended Rule 18 ([Energetica India](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/ministry-of-power-notifies-rules-to-expand-energy-storage-systems-ownership-and-usage)), and stored energy can be traded on exchanges, but the obstacles are specific. The DAM price cap was argued in CERC hearings in June 2026 to render storage participation "commercially unviable" ([CERC ROP 194/MP/2026](https://cercind.gov.in/2026/ROP/194-MP-2026_230626.pdf)); IEX's storage-only Peak DAM/RTM petition is undecided and was criticised by CER-IITK as an artificial entry barrier ([CER-IITK](https://cer.iitk.ac.in/odf_assets/upload_files/blog_CER-IITK_Comments_on_IEX_Petition_for_Peak_DAM_and_Peak_RTM1.pdf)); Rajasthan's May 2026 draft procedure bars grid charging of co-located storage ([JMK](https://jmkresearch.com/indias-emerging-merchant-bess-market-faces-its-first-regulatory-test/)), which directly undercuts the "absorb excess from plants, release later" concept unless the battery sits behind the plant's own meter; IEX activated TRAS trading in June 2023 but recorded no participation ([CEEW copy of RERC BESS regulations](https://ceep.co.in/storage/publication/rerc-battery-energy-storage-systems-regulations.pdf?r=oXRmuL)), and the only battery ancillary precedent is a 150 MW / 300 MWh slice of a Grid-India pilot ([POSOCO](https://posoco.in/english-approved-procedure-for-scheduling-metering-accounting-and-settlement-for-ancillary-portion-150mw-300mwh-of-bess-pilot-project)); the draft IEGC amendment sets standalone ESS trial runs at a 50 MW minimum ([Indian Infrastructure](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2026/09/23/cerc-issues-draft-cerc-indian-electricity-grid-code-second-amendment-regulations-2026/)). Most important, contracted capacity is not aggregatable: SECI agreements require 95% availability and up to two cycles a day with liquidated damages of twice the monthly capacity charge on shortfall ([SECI](https://www.seci.co.in/Upload/Tender/SECI000069-9112235-Clarifications-01-ESS-finalupload.pdf)), so the pool an aggregator can touch is the merchant fleet, C&I behind-the-meter systems and EV-swap batteries. The curtailment-absorption pitch is also smaller than it sounds: at 470 GWh a quarter the notes value lost energy at roughly INR 118 crore a quarter, consistent with the Rajasthan loss claim, and one estimate says about 10 GWh of batteries would eliminate current curtailment ([TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/storage/india-needs-10-gwh-battery-storage-to-cut-re-curtailment)); the prize is the INR 5.5-8.6/kWh spread, not the curtailed volume.

Competition is asset-heavy and unfunded on the software side. JSW Energy has 29.6 GWh of locked-in storage and its own 5 GWh/yr assembly plant ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/magazine/deep-dive/story/how-jsw-energy-is-building-round-the-clock-renewable-power-solutions-536794-2026-06-15)); Greenko sells "cloud storage" from pumped hydro, including a 1,500 MWh offtake with Serentica ([Serentica](https://www.serenticaglobal.com/serentica-renewables-announces-groundbreaking-energy-storage-contract-with-greenko/)); Sheru aggregates about 20 MWh of EV-swap batteries ([T&D India](https://www.tndindia.com/there-is-immense-scope-for-batteries-to-support-the-power-grid-sheru/)); VoltSeal raised USD 1.5 million for C&I behind-the-meter LFP with a planned VPP layer ([Mercom](https://mercomindia.com/energy-storage-startup-voltseal-raises-1-5m-pre-seed)); and total Indian energy-storage-tech VC funding in 2025 was only about **USD 136 million** ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/explore/energy-storage-tech-startups-in-india/__YDj182z24H2eTlzTnKObBNoe3XCBh5ACzNG0w9VGxQs)). No independent Habitat- or Arenko-style optimiser with Indian market access exists. Failure modes beyond saturation are counterparty and policy: CERC cancelled SECI's first standalone BESS tender in January 2025 after a 245-day PPA lag ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/cerc-rejects-seci-s-first-battery-storage-project-over-2-year-delay-125010500359_1.html)), DISCOMs owed generators INR 581 billion in June 2025 ([Mercom](https://mercomindia.com/discoms-owed-power-generators-581-billion-june-2025)), storage-specific late-payment protection is only a November 2026 draft ([Power Peak Digest](https://powerpeakdigest.com/?p=71271)), and the Moss Landing fire destroyed about 80% of a 300 MW / 1,200 MWh phase ([Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/fire-at-moss-landing-energy-storage-facility-what-we-know-so-far)), a reminder that promising availability on batteries one does not control carries pass-through penalty risk.

## Recommendation: wedge through generator DSM savings, scale into storage optimisation

The sequence that fits the evidence is Play 2 first, Play 3 as the scale business, and Play 1 only as a later B2B software line. The wedge is an outcome-priced deviation-reduction and dispatch service for wind, solar and hybrid generators, sold as a share of DSM charges avoided rather than a per-MW fee, because that is the only pricing that escapes the INR 4,000/MW commodity and because the regulator has just handed every ISTS-connected RE plant a quantifiable, rising cost. The first target segment is wind and hybrid plants in Rajasthan, Gujarat and the Southern region, where the ±10% band bites hardest and where NGEL-type block data shows 40% of blocks already out of band; the pitch is pooling-station aggregation (which Tata Power says cuts DSM materially), 16-revisions-a-day intraday rescheduling, and RTM rebalancing of forecast error, with a measured, audited baseline so the generator pays only on verified savings. Delivery should run through a trader partner such as PTC, Tata Power Trading or Manikaran Power as a client under the Power Market Regulations, deferring the INR 2 crore Category V licence until the book justifies it; the trader gets volume, the startup gets exchange access, Form IV settlement plumbing and credibility with lenders. The data moat is built the same way: as a QCA or sub-QCA the startup obtains SLDC/RLDC schedule, DSM and curtailment data that no public API provides, and the plant-SCADA integrations needed for forecasting are the same ones needed to dispatch a co-located battery later.

Play 3 then follows the generator relationships rather than preceding them. The 2026-28 commissioning wave, the merchant fleet IEX describes, and the CEA's proposal to mandate 10% co-located storage in new solar and wind tenders from 2027 ([TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/storage/india-may-mandate-storage-solar-wind-projects-2027)) mean that within two years many of the startup's forecasting clients will own batteries they do not know how to monetise. The product becomes a route-to-market and optimisation contract in the GB mould, revenue-share first, floor-plus-share once there is a track record, with the floor sized to the contracted tariff equivalent (the notes' INR 1.1-1.4 lakh per MWh-of-duration per month benchmark across BESS and pumped-storage awards is a useful anchor) and the upside shared from spread capture. The co-located, behind-the-pooling-station-meter structure sidesteps Rajasthan's grid-charging ban and is where curtailed energy can legitimately be absorbed; standalone merchant assets are the second pool, and C&I behind-the-meter batteries a third once state DR regulations settle. This is also the version of the business investors have paid for elsewhere: Voltus, which sells aggregated flexibility to large B2B counterparties, raised a USD 225 million Series D in October 2026 ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/voltus-raises-225-million-series-d-led-by-generation-investment-management-to-scale-grid-flexibility/)), Quinbrook bought Habitat to sweat its own batteries, and infrastructure funds have put nine-figure sums into developer-operators with in-house optimisation. In India the likely early capital is development-finance and climate funds (BII, Norfund and IndiGrid's USD 300 million EnerGrid BESS platform ([Mercom](https://mercomindia.com/indigrid-bii-and-norfund-to-launch-300-million-platform-for-bess-projects)), Avaana's USD 135 million fund, Transition VC's INR 700 crore fund), and the realistic exit is a strategic tuck-in by an IPP or trader, so the business should be built to be profitable on revenue share rather than on a software multiple.

Play 1 is a watchlist item with a defined trigger. If PM Surya Ghar 2.0 adds a battery incentive, if the Rights of Consumers Amendment Rules 2026 are notified and two or more large SERCs define DR-provider eligibility, and if Maharashtra's DFPO or Rajasthan's aggregator registration produces a third-party aggregator contract with settlement, then the right move is to license the dispatch and settlement engine already built for Play 3 to DISCOMs and PM Surya Ghar installers, who own the customer relationship and can enrol batteries by default. Building a consumer-facing aggregator before those triggers would replicate Swell's capital structure in a market with a fraction of Swell's per-household value.

## Open questions and the next 90 days of validation

The most important unknowns are empirical and testable. First, the DSM recovery rate: no Indian vendor publishes audited savings, so the startup should obtain 15-minute schedule-versus-actual data from three to five friendly wind and hybrid plants, replay FY26 under the ±5%/±10% bands and the FY27 X-factor, and measure how much of the charge a better intraday forecast and pooling would have avoided; if the answer is below 20%, outcome pricing does not work and the wedge needs rethinking. Second, willingness to pay: the current QCA circular rates for FY26-27 were not found, and the hypothesis that generators will pay 20-30% of verified savings needs five signed letters of intent, including at least one from an IPP with an in-house team. Third, access: the trader-client route is legal in principle but the commercial terms (margin split, who holds the IEX member relationship, Form IV responsibilities) and the existence or absence of any IEX member bidding API need to be confirmed with two traders. Fourth, the storage pool: verify IEX's single-source claim that about 80% of commissioned BESS is merchant, obtain a list of standalone and co-located assets with GNA and no SECI/DISCOM BESPA, and test whether any owner would sign a revenue-share optimisation pilot for a 2026 commissioning. Fifth, regulatory watch items that could move the thesis in either direction within twelve months: the final text of the Power Market (Second Amendment) Regulations and the coupling go-live date, CERC's decision on Peak DAM/RTM and any DAM cap change, the fate of Rajasthan's grid-charging ban, the notification status of the consumer-rights rules and the draft NEP 2026, the 5-minute RTM paper, and the Electricity (Amendment) Bill 2025, whose post-January 2026 status the notes could not establish. Sixth, the storage-capacity baseline itself, since 1.08 GWh, 5.3 GWh and 8.5 GWh cannot all be right and the size of the aggregatable pool depends on which is.

## Limitations of this research

Every figure in this report comes from web-search result extracts gathered on 14 November 2026; the session's network policy blocked direct fetches of regulator sites (cercind.gov.in, powermin.gov.in, cea.nic.in, grid-india.in) and most Indian and international trade-press domains (Mercom, Power Line, pv magazine India, JMK, IEEFA, Ember, Modo Energy, Energy-Storage.news, Latitude Media), so no regulation or report was read in full and several planned verification queries did not run. Figures explicitly derived by the researchers rather than sourced include the F&S fee pool, per-100 MW DSM and curtailment losses, merchant battery INR/MW/yr, contracted paybacks and the per-household Indian flexibility value; these are labelled as inference above and should be re-derived from primary data before use in a business plan. Single-source figures include IEX's "80% merchant" claim, Tata Power-DDL's 560 MW cumulative reduction (a secondary blog), the Uplight and Power Ledger pilot results (vendor-sourced) and sonnen's reported insolvency. Known conflicts left unresolved are the Category I trading-licence net worth (INR 75 crore versus INR 100 crore), whether 2025 curtailment was compensated (Ember) or borne by developers (Rajasthan industry), India's operational BESS stock (1.08, 5.3 or 8.5 GWh), the TRAS commencement date, and the ConnectedSolutions battery rate. Several named players (Enphase, Piclo, Statkraft's VPP economics, Manikaran Power volumes, Ampin, Hero Future Energies) and topics (IEX membership fees and API, SRAS/TRAS cleared volumes, FY26-27 QCA rates, electricity-futures volumes, an Indian BESS fire record) were not covered because the search budget was exhausted.

## Conclusion

The deeper finding is that India's regulators are building the flexibility market from the top down, generators first, storage second, consumers last, and a startup's sequencing should mirror that order rather than fight it. The binding instruments of 2024-26 all make a generator's hourly accuracy and a battery's hourly availability more valuable, while the instruments that would monetise a household's flexibility remain drafts whose delivery date is unknowable. That asymmetry turns the unglamorous forecasting-and-scheduling niche, which has twice ended in small strategic acquisitions, into a different business when priced on outcomes and extended into storage dispatch: the same plant integrations, SLDC data and trader relationships that cut a wind farm's DSM bill in 2027 are the ones needed to run its co-located battery into the INR 8.60/kWh spread in 2028.

The strategic risk is not that the opportunity is too small but that its best years are front-loaded. Every benchmark market shows merchant storage revenue falling 50-85% within two to three years of fleet build-out, and IEX itself gives the Indian evening-peak window three to five years. A founder who enters through generators now, converts merchant upside into floor-plus-share contracts before the spread compresses, and holds the residential play in reserve until a battery subsidy and a notified aggregator status arrive will own the data and relationships that the eventual capacity market, 5-minute settlement and DISCOM flexibility obligations reward, whichever of those drafts becomes law first.
